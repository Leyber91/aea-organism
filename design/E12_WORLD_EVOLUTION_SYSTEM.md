# E12 — THE WORLD EVOLUTION SYSTEM

*How every world after the first gets its look. Written 2026-07-26, after World 1 shipped and after*
*the first draft of World 2 made the mistake this file exists to prevent.*

**The mistake:** World 2's brief opened with *"THE MATERIAL, LOCKED, IDENTICAL TO WORLD 1."* That is a
repaint. A player who travels somewhere must arrive somewhere. Continuity is not sameness.

**The rule:** a new world is a **generation** of the old one, and the generation is driven by what the
architecture gained — never by a palette.

---

## 1 · THE EVOLUTION AXIS. What each world adds to the universe.

Each world seats a class of AEA components, and each class gives the *universe itself* a faculty it did
not have. That faculty is the whole brief for the visual evolution. Everything else follows from it.

| world | class · layer | components | THE FACULTY THE UNIVERSE GAINS | what that does to the world physically |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | ANSWERER · SPECIFY | GOAL, METHOD, MANNER | **it can answer.** One thing in, one thing out, nothing else | sealed one-way bodies; ground that rises only where a good question was asked; amber = a connection made |
| 2 | CORRECTOR · REPAIR | MEASURE, READOUT, VALIDATION, CLOCK, CRITIC, LADDER, COUNCIL | **it can look at itself.** A second thing observes the first | there is now an observer *in* the world; nothing is free; the colour that meant "working" becomes something to check |
| 3 | REMEMBERER · REMEMBER | CHECKPOINT, RECALL, CONVERSATION | **it can carry.** What happened survives the exchange | traces persist; things accumulate; the world stops resetting behind the player |
| 4 | KEEPER · PERSIST | *unbuilt* | **it can continue.** It runs while nobody is watching | the world is doing something when the player arrives, and was before |
| 5 | HAND · ACT | *unbuilt* | **it can reach.** It changes things outside itself | the world touches the ground rather than only occupying it |
| 6 | APPRENTICE · CHANGE | *unbuilt* | **it can rewrite itself.** Its own body is editable | the world is under construction by its own inhabitants |

**Write the faculty in the brief and let the model design from it.** "It can look at itself" is a
generative instruction. "Add cyan" is not.

**The honesty constraint on this table:** worlds 4, 5 and 6 have no measured components, so their
faculties are stated as *design intent* and must be labelled as such in any brief. Do not draw a world
whose capability has never been run.

---

## 2 · THE REFERENCE RULE. Exactly three images, every time.

The model must **see** the previous world, not read a description of it. Three rounds of prose
description produced beige machines; the first round with images produced the system we shipped.

Attach exactly these three, no more:

| slot | which image | what it carries |
|---|---|---|
| **THE ANCHOR** | the previous world's **DOOR creature**, hero shot | the subject to be redrawn. Its meaning changes in the new world, which is the point |
| **THE PLACE** | the previous world's strongest **world shot** | ground, air, light, scale, atmosphere |
| **THE EDGE** | the previous world's **boundary shot** | where the player was standing when they left |

Three is the cap for a reason. More references average the model toward a mean; these three fix
subject, environment and transition and leave everything else free to move.

**For World 2** that is `09_speciosus_operis_THE_DOOR.png`, `S3_the_terrain.png`, `S4_the_edge.png`.

---

## 3 · THE ANCHOR RULE. Always the door creature.

The anchor is the single creature redrawn in all three candidate evolutions, so the three are
comparable. **Use the previous world's door creature.**

It is the right choice every time because it is the creature the player met last, it is the reason they
moved on, and **the new world is defined by being the place where that creature can finally be
addressed.** Redrawing it in the new style states the whole transition on one subject.

Same pose, same scale, same light state across all three candidates. Only the system changes.

*(World 2's brief originally anchored on `Integer sufficiens` because it is a clean, closed, simple test
surface. That is the lesser goal. `Speciosus operis` carries meaning and its difficult lighting is a
feature: it tests whether the new system can still carry the light law.)*

---

## 4 · THE TURN STRUCTURE. Nine turns per world.

| turn | deliverable |
|---|---|
| **1** | **THREE EVOLUTIONS.** The anchor creature, three ways, with the three references attached. Two or three sentences of reasoning before each: what changed and what it says about the new faculty. **No other creature. No world shot.** Fence everything else off with an explicit STOP. |
| **2** | **THE PICK, AND THE PROOF.** Redraw the *second* bridge creature in the chosen style. Seeing two familiar animals in the new system is what confirms it is an evolution and not a different game. |
| 3-6 | **THE NEW CREATURES**, one per message, hardest first. |
| 7 | **THE PLACE.** What this world feels like to be in. |
| 8 | **THE DOOR.** The creature this world cannot solve. |
| 9 | **THE EDGE**, which becomes the next world's reference. |

---

## 5 · WHAT CARRIES OVER UNCHANGED, AND MUST BE RESTATED EVERY TIME

These are not style. They are the game's laws, and they survive every evolution:

- **It is a LIVING ANIMAL.** Anatomy, posture, weight, a front. Never an object, never terrain.
- **THE SILHOUETTE IS THE DIAGNOSIS.** Nameable as a black shape at thumbnail size.
- **A STATE NAMES A PART.** `OPEN` is a claim; `OPEN (THE METHOD)` is a measurement. Anything untested
  renders UNMEASURED: dark, with visibly empty sockets.
- **SCALE IS IN FRAME**, as a 1.8 m hairline human outline.
- **NO TEXT ANYWHERE.**
- **INVENT NO DECORATION.**
- **An absence cannot be drawn.** Write every absence as a positive surface with a real-world referent.
- **NO TWO BODY PLANS MAY COLLIDE**, across *all* worlds. Carry the running list into every brief. After
  World 1 that list is ten bodies and four of them are quadrupeds; the budget is spent.

---

## 6 · THE TWO FAILURE MODES TO WATCH FOR

**THE REPAINT.** The new world is the old world in a different colour. Test: can a player say what
changed *and why*, with the why coming out of the fiction? If the answer is "it is bluer", start again.

**THE DISCONTINUITY.** The new world is a different game. Test: put the anchor's old and new renders
side by side. Is it recognisably the same animal? If not, the evolution went too far and the player
will not believe they travelled — they will think they restarted.

Turn 2 exists to catch the second one before four creatures are rendered in a style that broke.
