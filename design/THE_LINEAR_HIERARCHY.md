# THE LINEAR HIERARCHY - all 86 census items, in the order necessity admits them

Generated by `scratchpad/gen_hierarchy.py`, which refuses to write unless the placement is an
exact partition of C-01..C-86: every item placed once, none dropped, none duplicated. The count
check is the point - a hierarchy that leaves items unplaced is a filing preference.

**THE PLACEMENT RULE.** An item sits at the lowest rung where it is both POSSIBLE (the machinery
it needs exists below it) and NECESSARY (the rung below cannot do it). Each rung is everything
above it plus its own items. Where an item is *used* higher up as well, the placement records
where it first becomes unavoidable.

Census disposition, quoted from the audit: **E** embodied - **C** compressed - **M** missing -
**O** out of scope.

**Two things the distribution says out loud.**

The first four rungs hold 22 of 86 items, and everything measured in this project so far lives
in them. The top two rungs hold 30, and none of it is reachable until the rungs below hold -
which is C-63's ordering claim, arrived at independently by asking what forces what.

L8 alone holds 21 items, a quarter of the architecture, and it is the part that proposes the
entity's own next capability. It sits last because an entity that cannot run unattended cannot
propose anything, and one that cannot remember cannot evaluate what it proposed.

**One item is not in the 86.** THE READOUT belongs at L1 and the census has no row for it. It
was found by watching a rod work rather than by auditing the architecture's own description, so
the 86 is a floor rather than a count. Any rung may gain items the same way.
