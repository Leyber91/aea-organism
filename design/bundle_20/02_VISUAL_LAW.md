# THE VISUAL LAW — absolute, no exceptions

## 1. Two inks, and only two

**VOID** — the ground of every image: `#050a10` deepening to `#0a1420`. Most of every frame.

**STRUCTURE** — cold blue-gray `rgba(120,155,175, x)` at exactly five opacities and no others:
`0.70` values and important lines · `0.45` labels · `0.28` secondary detail ·
`0.14` hairlines and frames · `0.08` faint rules and grids.

**LIFE** — amber, at exactly two tiers: `#ffb000` HOT (working right now) and `#d4a24c` WARM
(exists, idle, not working). Amber appears ONLY where something is alive, running, understood, or
earned. It is never decoration, never a highlight, never used to make a picture prettier.

**Forbidden absolutely**: red, green, white, teal, purple, any third hue, any gradient between
hues, any coloured glow other than amber. A single stray colour invalidates a sheet.

**The amber census**: on any finished sheet, count the amber. If more than roughly one tenth of
the marks are amber, the sheet is over-lit and wrong. Most sheets should have one or two amber
accents total. A cold sheet is a correct sheet.

## 2. The thesis behind the two inks

Amber is not a style choice — it is a variable, and the variable is UNDERSTANDING. A frame with
little amber is early in the story; a frame with more amber is later. The ink ratio of any image
is therefore a save file. This is why amber must be earned in every drawing.

## 3. Rendering

Flat, matte, technical. A screen-printed film poster or an engineering blueprint — never
photoreal, never glossy, never chrome, no lens flares, no bloom halos, no volumetric god-rays,
no depth-of-field blur used as decoration. Line weights are thin and deliberate: roughly 0.10mm
for detail, 0.18mm for structure. Surfaces read through line and value, not through material
shine.

## 4. The four owned motifs

1. **The concentric field** — rings radiating from a centre. The sacred motif. It appears etched
   in ground planes, in seal faces, in reticles, in title blocks.
2. **The live trace** — a single amber packet travelling along a thin wire. The game's autograph.
   Nothing else in this world moves light along a line.
3. **The six-glyph grammar** — circle, pentagon, hexagon, triangle, square, diamond. These are the
   ONLY icons in this world. No wrenches, no gears, no lightbulbs, no arrows-as-decoration, no
   representational pictograms of any kind. If an idea needs an icon, it must be built from these
   six shapes and abstract line-work.
4. **Terminal text as texture** — small monospaced labels, codes, and readouts used as a surface
   material. Denser text means more evidence, never more ornament.

## 5. Composition

Enormous negative space; the darkness is a material, not a background. Corner brackets instead of
boxes — frames are indicated by four corner ticks, never closed rectangles. Edge ruler scales.
A small diagram title block in one corner carrying an ID, revision, scale and status. Callouts
use thin leader lines to labelled points. Symmetry where the subject is ceremonial, asymmetry
where the subject is a landscape.

## 6. The tells — what makes an image look dated, and is therefore banned

Uniform grey fog · flat default material response with no value hierarchy · hard rectangular UI
boxes with borders on all four sides · lens flares and sprite-sheet particle sparkles · visible
uniform floor grids of the default-3D-viewport kind · saturated accent colours on near-black ·
chrome and specular highlights · centred symmetrical compositions with no negative space ·
drop shadows · bevelled edges · anything that reads as a 2003 game menu.

## 7. The tells of the current era, which we want

Authored gradients rather than flat fills · dithering and fine grain used as texture ·
silhouette-first shape design where forms read at a glance in pure black · confident oversized
typography with generous letter-spacing · rigorous palette discipline · fog used as an
art-direction tool that separates depth bands · asymmetric compositions with weighted emptiness ·
line-work that carries the detail instead of texture maps.

## 8. Depth

Three bands must always separate: near objects at full contrast, middle objects at roughly forty
percent, far objects as pure silhouette dissolving into a sky of the same colour as the fog.
A greyscale copy of any landscape sheet must still show three distinct depths.
