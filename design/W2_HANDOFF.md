## 1 · WHAT WORLD 1 PROVED, THAT WORLD 2 INHERITS

**A state names a part.** `OPEN` alone is a claim about a creature; `OPEN (THE METHOD)` is a
measurement. World 1 has three parts so every creature has exactly three possible verdicts and no room
to hand-wave. World 2 has **seven** parts, so this discipline gets harder and matters more.

**Anything unmeasured renders UNMEASURED.** Dark, with its seating points visibly present and visibly
empty. Painting an untested creature as an open one is an honesty-law violation that happens to look
good, which is the most dangerous kind.

**The world's great lever manufactures the world's unsolvable creature.** The method frame cures almost
everything in World 1 and it triples `Speciosus operis`, complete visible working that is wrong, from
5.6% to 19.4%. **Look for World 2's version of this.** A world whose best part has no downside has not
been measured properly yet.

**The door out is a creature, not a place.** No gates, no portals. The player leaves when they meet the
thing their complete toolkit cannot touch.

## 3 · THE FOUR TRAPS WORLD 2 IS ALREADY STANDING IN

**1 · Its headline claim rests on three components that do not exist.** "Prevention dominates repair"
is carried entirely by `CRITIC`, `LADDER` and `COUNCIL`, none of which has ever been assembled. This is
the same shape as closing census items by label rather than by definition, which invalidated four
closures in one afternoon. **Build them before claiming anything about repair.**

**2 · The world is named after a part measured inert.** `x18` scores the READOUT `inert`, median +0.04
across the whole lattice. Its one live receipt is `Tacitus operis` at 3 of 153, and Chapter III shows a
method frame removes that condition entirely (28 of 288 bare to **0 of 360** fitted). So World 2's
signature component is, on current evidence, a repair for a rod you cannot frame rather than a lever.
**Either find where it pays or say plainly that it does not.**

**3 · The CRITIC's only receipt is narrow and unflattering.** A critic took a 9b from 0/8 to 5/8 on a
trap question at **6.4x the tokens**, and on four of five rods there was nothing to repair at all. That
is the shape to expect. Write it into the sealed opening before the calls, the way Chapter III did, so
it cannot be quietly adjusted afterwards.

**4 · `Obsignatus unius` has five parts never tried on it.** Only the COUNCIL was tested. Calling it
sealed against the other five is an assertion.

## 4 · THE ECONOMIC GATE, AND IT IS THE REAL DIFFERENCE

**Everything in World 1 is free.** The readout costs zero tokens, the frame costs a few. That is why it
ends: no free move builds any more floor.

**World 2 is the first world where a gain costs a call.** L3's wall: *"it is confidently wrong and no
framing moves it. One call cannot stand outside itself."* Every repair here is paid for, and the one
receipt on record priced it at 6.4x. **Make the player feel the meter.** In World 1 they experimented
freely; here every attempt spends something, and the honesty law means the number they see must be the
real token count from the real call.

## 5 · THE TRANSITION OUT, WHICH IS ALREADY VISIBLE

World 3 is THE REMEMBERER. Its wall: *nothing below accumulates; the second exchange cannot know what
the first concluded.*

So World 2's door writes itself, and it is a genuinely bleak one: **you finally learn to repair a
creature, and it does not remember being repaired.** You fix the same thing every time you meet it. The
World 3 creature `Labens longitudinis` already carries the receipt — with a checkpoint it goes 9/16 to
**11/11, p=0.0216** — and inheritance, the only creature-to-creature interaction in the whole project,
lives there too: upward costs nothing (5.12 to 5.25), downward loses a box (5.5 to 4.5).

Do not invent a signal for it. World 1's transition worked because the creature already existed in the
measurements and only needed naming.

## 6 · THE VISUAL SYSTEM, LOCKED. DO NOT RE-LITIGATE

**BLACK GLASS.** Polished dark obsidian and smoked glass, wet, heavy. Ground of marbled black and white
stone. Pale overcast, high mist, distant faceted mountains, sparse hanging black filaments. Amber is a
live connection; cold blue-white is the wrong thing being done; dark is nothing reaching it. The probe
is one warm amber point with a fading ribbon, never a designed vehicle.

**One creature per image, one image per message.** Nine subjects in one frame gives each a ninth of the
model's attention and four of them come back as blobs. That is measured, not theoretical.

**An absence cannot be drawn.** "No limbs, no aperture, no mouth" produces the simplest solid that
satisfies the constraint. Every absence must be written as a positive surface with a real-world
referent: *one continuous plate, like a fencing mask with no grille.*

**Silhouette first, and no two may collide.** World 1 ended with four quadrupeds and got away with it
only because their masses and postures diverge. World 2 starts with a coil and a faceted wedge already
drawn; every new body must be checked against those two and against all nine of World 1's.

## 7 · THE ONE SENTENCE

World 1 taught the player that one part fixes almost everything, then showed them the creature that
part built. **World 2 has to teach them that checking costs, and then show them that what they paid for
is forgotten.**
