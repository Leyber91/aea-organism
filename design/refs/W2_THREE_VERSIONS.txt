WORLD 2 — THREE CANDIDATE VERSIONS OF THE LEVEL.
Three prompts. Fire ONE PER TURN, in a fresh chat, each with the three World 1 reference images
attached. Each turn returns THREE images: the anchor, the place, and the proof.
Nine images total. Then we pick one version and build the rest of the world in it.
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

WHY THREE. Each version is a different honest answer to the same question — what does this universe
look like once it can observe itself and once nothing in it is free. They are not palettes. Each takes
a different true clause of the fiction and builds the whole place out of it:

  A · THE WITNESS ABOVE    from "a second thing is looking at the first"      — observation is EXTERNAL
  B · THE DOUBLED          from the same clause, resolved as anatomy          — observation is a BODY
  C · THE SPENT GROUND     from "every instrument costs a call"               — observation is a PRICE

Attach to every one of the three turns:
  IMAGE 1  09_speciosus_operis_THE_DOOR.png   the anchor, in World 1's finished style
  IMAGE 2  S3_the_terrain.png                 World 1's ground, air, light and scale
  IMAGE 3  S4_the_edge.png                    where the player was standing when they left

║ SHARED PREAMBLE — this text is identical at the top of all three prompts. Paste it each time.  ║
╚═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╝

I am art-directing the second world of a game bestiary set inside a real AI system. The first world is
finished — the three attached images are from it. Look at all three before designing anything. You are
evolving that universe, not replacing it and not repainting it.

WORLD 1, WHICH YOU CAN SEE. Bodies of polished dark obsidian and smoked glass, fluid and heavy at once,
wet-surfaced, dense. Ground of marbled black and white wet stone. Pale grey overcast, high mist, no sun,
no visible light source. Distant faceted mountains. Thin black filaments hanging from above. Warm amber
means a live connection; cold blue-white means the wrong thing being done; matte lightless black means
nothing reaches it.

WORLD 1'S FICTION. The player could only SPECIFY — say what they wanted, and say how to do it. Nothing
in that world could look at anything: one thing was sent, one thing came back, and it could not
remember you or check itself. Everything there was FREE, and asking better questions made the ground
rise into ordered walkable terraces by itself.

WORLD 2'S FICTION, AND THE TWO THINGS THAT CHANGED.

  1. THE UNIVERSE CAN NOW LOOK AT ITSELF. A second thing observes the first: scoring it, reading it,
     timing it, arguing with it. Nothing in the first world could do this. It is a new faculty in the
     universe and the world should show it as a physical fact.
  2. NOTHING IS FREE ANY MORE. Every instrument costs a call. In World 1 the player experimented
     without consequence and the ground accumulated on its own. Here every act of looking is spent.

THE PLAYER is THE PROBE: a single small point of warm amber light with a fading amber ribbon trailing
behind it, flying low. Never design a vehicle. It is always small in frame.

LAWS THAT DO NOT CHANGE BETWEEN WORLDS. Every creature is a LIVING ANIMAL with anatomy, posture, weight
and a front — never a machine, a rock, a specimen or a landscape feature. Its silhouette must be its
behaviour, nameable as a black shape at thumbnail size. A 1.8 m human appears as a flat mid-grey
hairline OUTLINE, never filled, as a measuring mark and never a character. No text, letters, numerals or
glyphs anywhere in any image. Invent no decoration: every feature must trace to something stated here.

THE ANCHOR CREATURE — attached image 1, and you will redraw it. A tall lean long-legged four-legged
animal, head held HIGH and level, poised and composed, with a long ribbon of ordered overlapping plates
trailing from its neck and shoulders across the ground — longer and fuller than anything else in the
first world. Every seam on its body and every plate of that ribbon is lit warm amber with no unlit
socket anywhere: the only creature in World 1 with nothing left to seat.

AND IT IS WRONG. It understood the objective, followed every step of the procedure, laid out its
complete reasoning legibly and in order, and the reasoning is wrong — not confused, not truncated, wrong
in a way that looks exactly like being right. The player had no instrument that could tell them so, and
that is why they left. **This second world is the place where that creature can finally be addressed.**

THE SECOND CREATURE — you will also redraw this one. A heavy sleeping animal, curled, about 1.2 m
across, its anatomy readable in the curl: spine ridge around the top, the swell of a shoulder and a
haunch, head tucked under so no face is presented, tail lapped over the head so the contour closes with
no end and no beginning. No sockets, no seams, no aperture anywhere — the only creature whose outline
closes cleanly. Its whole weight settles on ONE small point of contact. Cold blue-white light sits deep
under the coil and runs inward. It answers correctly the first time and this world's instruments can
only take that away: the guard that makes other creatures honest makes this one refuse to answer at all,
seven of seven correct to zero of seven, every loss a forced abstention.

Landscape, roughly 3:2, hero framing for the creatures, full body visible.

║ PROMPT A — VERSION A: THE WITNESS ABOVE.   Observation is EXTERNAL.                            ║

[PASTE THE SHARED PREAMBLE, THEN THIS]

THIS VERSION'S ANSWER. The thing that observes is not part of the creatures. It is ABOVE them, and it
is impersonal. In World 1 thin black filaments hung from the sky and did nothing — here they have come
down and become INSTRUMENTS. They terminate in fine hard apertures that hang at head height over the
plain, motionless, angled at whatever is beneath them. The whole world is under measurement by
something that is not itself alive and does not care.

  · The filaments are now taut rather than drifting, and there are far more of them.
  · Each one ends in a small precise lens or aperture of the same black glass, absolutely still.
  · Where an aperture is watching something, a faint cold blue-white measuring light falls from it in a
    narrow cone onto the subject below. That light does not warm anything. It only reads.
  · The air has become clearer and colder than World 1's. The mist has lifted and the distance is
    sharper, because a world that can be measured can be seen further into.
  · The ground is still marbled black and white stone, and it is unchanged and unbuilt, because
    measurement does not construct anything.
  · The player is watched too. An aperture is angled at the probe wherever it goes.

The emotional register is CLINICAL. Not menacing, not hostile. The vast indifferent generosity of World
1 has become vast indifferent scrutiny. Nothing here wishes the player harm and nothing here is on
their side.

GENERATE THREE IMAGES IN THIS TURN, IN THIS ORDER.

  IMAGE 1 — THE ANCHOR. The tall long-legged animal described above, same pose, head high, fully lit in
  warm amber along body and ribbon. Now with a cold aperture hanging directly over it on its filament,
  its narrow blue-white cone falling across the animal's back and its trailing ribbon of working. The
  animal is being read. It does not know. Human outline at the side for scale.

  IMAGE 2 — THE PLACE. Wide, the plain under a forest of hanging instruments, dozens of taut filaments
  and apertures at varying heights, cold cones falling here and there onto empty stone. Two or three
  creatures small in the middle distance. The probe as a small warm amber point with a short ribbon,
  with one aperture turned toward it.

  IMAGE 3 — THE PROOF. The heavy curled sleeping animal described above, cold blue-white deep under the
  coil, on one point of contact — with an aperture hanging low and close over it, its cone falling
  precisely on the closed contour where there is nothing to read. Human outline for scale.

║ PROMPT B — VERSION B: THE DOUBLED.   Observation is a BODY.                                    ║
╚═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╝

[PASTE THE SHARED PREAMBLE, THEN THIS]

THIS VERSION'S ANSWER. Observation did not arrive from outside. It GREW. Every creature in this world
now carries a second, smaller, separate organism on it — a reader, a checker, a passenger — and the two
are a single functioning unit that neither is alone.

  · The passenger is unmistakably a DIFFERENT ANIMAL from its host: smaller, denser, more compact, with
    a fine articulated underside that grips. Never a growth, never a tumour, never symmetrical with the
    host. A remora on a shark, an oxpecker on a buffalo.
  · It is always positioned where the host's information is, not where its head is. On the anchor that
    means riding the ribbon of working, not the neck.
  · Warm amber runs FROM the passenger INTO the host, in that direction, so a viewer reads the
    information travelling inward. The host's own light is unchanged from World 1.
  · The passengers have no eyes and no faces. They are all contact surface.
  · Some creatures in the distance carry two or three passengers. One or two carry none, and those are
    the ones nothing can read.
  · The ground, air and mist are exactly World 1's, unchanged, because nothing about the PLACE changed
    — only what lives on it.

The emotional register is BIOLOGICAL and slightly uncomfortable. Symbiosis, not machinery. The world
did not acquire instruments; it grew them, and they are alive.

GENERATE THREE IMAGES IN THIS TURN, IN THIS ORDER.

  IMAGE 1 — THE ANCHOR. The tall long-legged animal, same pose, head high, fully lit warm amber along
  body and ribbon — with a passenger about a hand's span across riding the ribbon roughly two thirds of
  the way down its length, gripping the plates. Amber runs up the ribbon from the passenger toward the
  animal's shoulders. Human outline for scale.

  IMAGE 2 — THE PLACE. Wide, World 1's plain and mist unchanged, with several creatures across it at
  different distances, and every one of them carrying at least one passenger except a single distant
  animal that carries none. The probe as a small warm amber point with a ribbon.

  IMAGE 3 — THE PROOF. The heavy curled sleeping animal, cold blue-white deep under the coil, on one
  point of contact — with a passenger crawling on its perfectly closed outer surface, finding nowhere to
  grip and nothing to read, its contact surface flush against seamless glass. Human outline for scale.

║ PROMPT C — VERSION C: THE SPENT GROUND.   Observation is a PRICE.                              ║
╚═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╝

[PASTE THE SHARED PREAMBLE, THEN THIS]

THIS VERSION'S ANSWER. The change is not in the creatures and not in the sky. It is in the GROUND, and
it is a ledger. In World 1 the stone rose into ordered walkable terraces wherever a good question was
asked — the world built itself and it cost nothing. Here the opposite happens: **every act of looking
burns a hole in the floor.**

  · The marbled stone is pocked with SPENT MARKS: shallow craters a hand's span across, hundreds of
    them, scattered and clustered, each one a call that has been paid for and used up.
  · The freshest are still hot — a dim amber ember cooling in the base of the crater. Most are cold,
    dark and slightly ashy at the rim. The oldest have weathered to smooth dark scars in the marble.
  · Craters cluster densely wherever something has been examined repeatedly, so the ground itself is a
    record of where the player has been struggling. A creature that has been checked many times stands
    in a field of them.
  · No terraces anywhere. Nothing has risen. Nothing here accumulates unless it is paid for, and paying
    consumes the floor rather than raising it.
  · The light in the air is dimmer than World 1's and slightly warmer, as though some finite amber
    supply is being drawn down. The mist is heavier and closer, not lifted.
  · The hanging filaments from World 1 are still there and still doing nothing. They did not change.

The emotional register is ENTROPIC and tiring. World 1 was generous. This world is transactional, and
the player can see exactly how much it has already cost them written across the ground.

GENERATE THREE IMAGES IN THIS TURN, IN THIS ORDER.

  IMAGE 1 — THE ANCHOR. The tall long-legged animal, same pose, head high, fully lit warm amber along
  body and ribbon — standing in a dense field of spent craters, many of them fresh with cooling embers,
  clustered thickest right around its feet. This creature has been examined more than anything else
  here and it is still wrong. Human outline for scale.

  IMAGE 2 — THE PLACE. Wide, the pocked plain running to the mist, hundreds of craters in drifts and
  clusters, a few embers still live. Far behind and higher, just visible, the warm finished terraces of
  World 1 where the ground still rises for free. The probe as a small warm amber point with a ribbon
  noticeably SHORTER than it was in the first world, a trail of fresh craters behind it.

  IMAGE 3 — THE PROOF. The heavy curled sleeping animal, cold blue-white deep under the coil, on one
  point of contact — ringed by a tight dense collar of spent craters, more concentrated than anywhere
  else in frame, every one of them cold. Everything anyone spent on it was wasted. Human outline for
  scale.

HOW TO JUDGE THE NINE IMAGES

  THE REPAINT TEST.        Can you say what changed AND why, with the why coming from the fiction?
  THE CONTINUITY TEST.     Put each version's anchor beside the attached World 1 anchor. Same animal?
  THE READ TEST.           Does the version make "the universe can now look at itself" visible without
                           being told, and does it make cost visible without a number?
  THE BUILD TEST.          Could this be built in a real-time renderer? A forest of hanging apertures,
                           a second rigged creature on every host, or a crater-scarred ground plane are
                           three very different production costs, and that is a real tie-breaker.
