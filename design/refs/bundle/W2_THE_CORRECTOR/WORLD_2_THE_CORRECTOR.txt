───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
PART A — CONTEXT, AND THE ONLY THING I WANT IN THIS TURN: THREE STYLE EVOLUTIONS.
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

I am art-directing the second world of a game bestiary. The first world is finished and its visual
system is locked. This message is shared context for everything that follows.

THE WORLD. THE CORRECTOR, the second region of a game set inside a real AI system.

In the first world the player could only SPECIFY: say what they wanted and say how to do it. Everything
there was free. They left it standing in front of a creature that had received every part they owned,
showed its complete reasoning, and was wrong — with no way to tell.

This world gives them the missing thing: instruments that LOOK AT AN ANSWER. Something that scores it,
something that reads the working, something that refuses to guess, something that watches the clock,
and something that argues with it.

AND EVERY ONE OF THEM COSTS A CALL. That is the difference between the two worlds and it must be felt.
In the first world the player experimented freely. Here, every attempt spends something.

WORLD 1'S VISUAL SYSTEM, WHICH IS FINISHED AND WHICH YOU ARE NOT COPYING.

  Bodies of polished dark obsidian and smoked glass: fluid and heavy at once, wet-surfaced, dense, with
  real weight. Ground of marbled black and white wet stone, flowing swirl patterns like currents frozen
  mid-move. Pale grey overcast sky, high mist, no sun, no visible light source. Distant faceted
  mountains half lost in haze. Thin black filaments hanging from above, sparse, drifting slightly.
  Museum-lit hero rendering of a living animal cast in black glass. Amber for a live connection, cold
  blue-white for the wrong thing being done, matte lightless black for nothing reaching it.

THE SECOND WORLD HAS TO FEEL LIKE SOMEWHERE THE PLAYER TRAVELLED TO, NOT A REPAINT OF THE FIRST.
Same universe, same species of thing, obviously continuous — and visibly further on. A player putting
the two side by side should be able to say what changed and why it changed, and the why should come out
of the fiction rather than out of a palette swap.

WHAT ACTUALLY CHANGED, AND THIS IS WHAT THE EVOLUTION SHOULD BE BUILT FROM:

  · IN WORLD 1 EVERYTHING WAS FREE. The player asked better questions and the ground rose into ordered
    walkable terraces by itself. Here every single instrument costs a call. Nothing accumulates unless
    it is paid for.
  · IN WORLD 1 NOTHING COULD LOOK AT ANYTHING. One thing was sent, one thing came back. Here there is
    a SECOND THING LOOKING AT THE FIRST — scoring it, reading it, timing it, arguing with it. The world
    has acquired an observer, and that is new to the universe.
  · IN WORLD 1 AMBER ALWAYS MEANT WORKING. Here one creature is perfectly, beautifully lit and its
    result is about nothing. The colour the player learned to trust becomes something they must check.
  · THE PLAYER IS NOT THE SAME EITHER. They arrived in World 1 carrying nothing. They leave it carrying
    instruments, and they arrive here able to see things they previously could not.

BEFORE ANY CREATURE IS DRAWN I WANT THREE POSSIBLE EVOLUTIONS OF THAT VISUAL SYSTEM.

Not three palettes. Three genuinely different answers to *what does this universe look like once it can
observe itself and once nothing is free.* They should differ in something structural — what the bodies
are made of now, what the ground does, what the light does, where the observer is, what the air is like
— while remaining unmistakably the same universe as the description above.

TO MAKE THEM COMPARABLE, RENDER EACH ONE ON THE SAME ANCHOR CREATURE — and I have attached three
images of the first world so you are evolving from what you can SEE rather than from my description
of it. Look at all three before you design anything.

  IMAGE 1, THE ANCHOR. The creature you will redraw three times. It is the one the player was standing
  in front of when the first world ended.
  IMAGE 2, THE PLACE. The first world's ground, air, light and scale.
  IMAGE 3, THE EDGE. Where the player was standing when they left.

THE ANCHOR, DESCRIBED SO YOU KNOW WHAT IT MEANS. A tall lean long-legged four-legged animal, head held
HIGH and level, poised and composed. A long ribbon of ordered overlapping plates trails from its neck,
back and shoulders across the ground behind it — longer, fuller and more orderly than any other in the
first world. It has done more work than anything there. Every seam on its body and every plate of that
ribbon is lit warm amber, evenly, with no unlit socket anywhere: it is the only creature in the first
world with nothing left to seat.

AND IT IS WRONG. It did everything the player asked. It understood the objective, it followed every
step of the procedure, it laid out its complete reasoning legibly and in order, and the reasoning is
wrong — not confused, not truncated, WRONG in a way that looks exactly like being right. The player had
no instrument that could tell them so, and that is why they left.

THIS SECOND WORLD IS THE PLACE WHERE THAT CREATURE CAN FINALLY BE ADDRESSED. Redrawing it in the new
system is the entire transition stated on one subject: same animal, same pose, same scale, same fully
lit amber state, in a universe that can now check it.

Give me three images, one per evolution, same creature, same pose, same scale, same fully lit amber
state. Before each, two or three sentences: what changed from World 1, and what that change says about
a world that can finally check its own answers.

I will pick one, and then everything below is rendered in it.

THE LIGHT LAW. A player diagnoses by colour before shape.
  amber            a live connection. A part is seated and working.
  cold blue-white  the wrong thing being done. Light present but running INWARD, never out into the air.
  dark             nothing reaches it. No filament, no glow, no rim light, no edge light, no separation
                   glow, no specular. A flat matte light-absorbing mass, the darkest value in frame, no
                   point brighter than the ground behind it. Read the form from the outline alone.
  empty sockets    open unfilled recesses in the glass, catching no light: a fitting nothing has ever
                   been seated in.

WORLD 2 BENDS THE LAW ONCE, DELIBERATELY, AND I WILL SAY WHERE. One creature here is fully lit and the
light is a lie. That inversion is the world's central lesson and it only works because the player has
spent a whole world learning to trust the colour.

SEVEN RULES. Repeated in every request.
  1. It is a LIVING ANIMAL. Anatomy, posture, weight, and a front it faces with. Not a machine, not a
     diagram, not a rock, not a specimen, not weather, not a landscape feature. If it could be mistaken
     for an object or terrain, the image has failed.
  2. THE SILHOUETTE IS THE DIAGNOSIS. Its body must BE its behaviour, nameable as a solid black shape
     at thumbnail size. I give you the outline signature to hit.
  3. SCALE IS IN FRAME. A 1.8 m human is present as a flat mid-grey hairline OUTLINE, never filled,
     upright, arms at its sides, no face, no hair, no clothing. A measuring mark, never a character.
  4. THE LIGHT LAW above, per creature. Never light one I have called dark.
  5. NO TEXT ANYWHERE. No names, labels, captions, letters, numerals, glyphs, watermarks, signatures.
  6. INVENT NO DECORATION. Every feature traces to something I stated.
  7. ONE CREATURE PER IMAGE unless I explicitly ask for a world shot.

BODIES ALREADY USED IN WORLD 1 — NOTHING HERE MAY REPEAT ONE. A vast limbless low-slung leviathan with
a forward-spilling apron; a small smooth leaning egg-body with one spur; a TALL LEAN LONG-LEGGED
QUADRUPED WITH ITS HEAD HUNG LOW and a ribbon of ranked plates trailing; the same body with its HEAD
HIGH; a lean quadruped with a channel milled along its topline; a heavy short-legged grazing ox with an
open mouth and no eyes; a small armless upright two-legged column; a small compact seated quadruped
with a tilted head; a heavy curled animal balanced on one point; a faceted standing wedge fused to the
ground. World 1 already spent four quadrupeds. **Only ONE creature in this world walks on four legs and
it is the first one, because it is an evolution of a World 1 animal and must be recognisable as such.**

Landscape, roughly 3:2, hero framing, full body visible, air around the subject.

ONCE A STYLE IS CHOSEN, ONE MORE FAMILIAR CREATURE GETS REDRAWN IN IT BEFORE ANY NEW ONE IS DESIGNED:
a heavy sleeping animal, curled, about 1.2 m across, its anatomy readable in the curl — spine ridge
around the top, the swell of a shoulder and a haunch, head tucked under, tail lapped over so the
contour closes with no end and no beginning. No sockets, no seams, no aperture anywhere; the only
creature whose outline closes cleanly. Its whole weight settles on ONE small point of contact. Cold
blue-white light deep under the coil, running inward. It answers correctly the first time and this
world's instruments can only take that away: the guard that makes other creatures honest makes this one
refuse to answer at all, seven of seven correct to zero of seven, every loss a forced abstention.

Two familiar animals in the new system is what proves this is an evolution and not a different game.

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
IMAGE 1 — LECTUS OPERIS, THE READ ONE.  amber. The first evolution in the game.
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

BEHAVIOUR. This is the World 1 creature that hung its head, laid out perfect reasoning, and had no
mouth to say it with — after an instrument is attached that lifts the answer out of the working. The
answer was already there. It was always there. It simply had no way out. One rod went from 0 of 8 to
**8 of 8 at zero additional cost**: nothing was added to the question, nothing was recomputed. Something
was finally read.

BODY. THE SAME ANIMAL AS BEFORE, and it must be instantly recognisable as that animal: tall, lean,
long-legged, narrow-bodied, with the same long ordered RIBBON of ranked overlapping plates trailing
from its neck, back and shoulders.

Two things have changed, and only two.

FIRST, ITS HEAD HAS COME UP. Not high and proud — level, and turned back over its own shoulder to look
along the ribbon at its own working. It is the first creature in the game to look at itself. The face
is still one continuous smooth closed plate with two eyes and no mouth. That has not been fixed and
never will be.

SECOND, THERE IS A READER ON THE RIBBON. A small separate organ, a hand's span across, of the same
black glass, riding on the ribbon about two thirds of the way down its length like a remora on a shark.
It is clearly a different creature or a different organ from the animal carrying it: smaller, denser,
with a fine articulated underside gripping the plates. It is not decorative and it is not attached to
the head. It sits on the working and it is reading it.

Warm amber runs UP the ribbon FROM the reader toward the animal's shoulders — the direction is
essential. The light travels from the working into the creature, not outward from the creature. The
answer is arriving from behind it.

SILHOUETTE SIGNATURE. The World 1 head-down animal, but with the head lifted and turned back along its
own body, and one small lump riding the trailing ribbon. In black those two changes are the whole
diagnosis: a backward-looking head and a passenger.

SCALE. 1.8 m at the shoulder, identical to the animal it evolved from. The human outline stands beside
it, shoulder level with the human's crown.

LIGHT. Amber, running up the ribbon from the reader to the shoulders and stopping at the neck. The head
itself stays dark. It has the answer and still cannot say it.


───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
IMAGE 2 — ARBITER VACUI, THE JUDGE OF THE VOID.  THIS IS WHERE THE LIGHT LAW IS BROKEN.
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

BEHAVIOUR. An instrument seated above a question that was never asked. It runs perfectly. It produces a
clean, confident, precise verdict, and the verdict is about nothing at all. With the objective removed,
50 of 158 trials still returned a number. Not one of them was right, and nothing downstream can tell
those numbers from real ones. **This is the most dangerous creature in the game.** Every other damaged
creature shows its damage in its result. This one is damaged and its result comes back immaculate.

BODY. A tall, elegant, long-legged WADING BIRD. Slender, poised, absolutely still, standing on two legs
in the shallow film of water across the marbled stone. Long neck held in a taut S-curve, drawn back and
ready. A long, fine, needle-straight beak, beautifully made, the most precise-looking thing anywhere in
this world.

It is the only two-legged walking creature in the game and the only one with a beak.

IT IS POISED TO STRIKE AT NOTHING. The beak is aimed down at a patch of bare, empty, featureless stone.
There is no prey, no ripple, no shadow, no mark, nothing whatsoever beneath it, and that emptiness must
be plainly visible and plainly untroubling to the bird. Its concentration is total and perfect and
directed at an empty patch of ground.

Every line of it says competence. Nothing about it is broken, ragged, damaged or wrong. That is the
entire point.

SILHOUETTE SIGNATURE. A poised wading bird: two thin legs, a coiled S-neck, a needle beak angled down.
Tall and narrow, the most vertical and most elegant shape in the game, and instantly distinct from
everything in World 1.

SCALE. 1.6 m tall standing. The human outline stands nearby, and the bird's eye is level with the
human's eye. It meets you as an equal, which makes it worse.

LIGHT — READ THIS TWICE. **This creature is fully, beautifully, perfectly lit in warm amber.** Every
seam glows. Every joint is live. It is the cleanest, healthiest, most obviously functional creature in
the entire game and there is not one cold or dark inch on it. The amber even pools faintly on the stone
beneath the beak, exactly where a verdict would land.

And it is aimed at nothing. The light is telling the truth about the instrument and lying about the
result. A player who has spent an entire world learning that amber means working should look at this
and feel the ground move.


───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
IMAGE 3 — TARDUS ERRORIS, THE SLOW ONE.  amber, and it ARRIVES LATE.
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

BEHAVIOUR. Its words lie and its clock does not. When it is about to be wrong it takes dramatically
longer — a median of 1.97 seconds when right against 16.12 seconds when wrong, more than eight times
slower. You cannot catch it by reading. You catch it by noticing how long it took. It is the first
creature in the game legible in a channel that contains no words at all. Another creature on the same
tasks shows a ratio of 0.95 and no signal whatsoever, so this tell belongs to the animal rather than to
the species.

BODY. A long, low, MANY-LEGGED animal, close to the ground, like a great armoured millipede or a
segmented isopod. Dozens of short legs in ordered ranks down both sides. Nothing else in the game has
more than four legs and nothing else is built from repeated identical segments in a straight line.

It is caught MID-STRIDE, walking slowly and deliberately from left to right, and its whole body is a
travelling wave: the legs on the near side are frozen in a smooth phase pattern, so a viewer can read
motion in a still image.

THE ONE THING THAT MAKES THIS CREATURE, AND IT MUST BE UNMISTAKABLE: **the light lags behind the body.**
The animal has moved and its amber has not caught up. Along its length, the glowing amber seams are
visibly OFFSET BACKWARD from the segments they belong to — displaced a segment or two toward the tail,
so the light traces where the body was rather than where it is. Behind the last segment the amber
continues alone for a short distance in empty air, a fading after-image of a creature that has already
passed. The body is sharp and the light is late.

Nothing about the body itself is damaged, marked or unusual. The lag is the only tell and it is not in
the anatomy.

SILHOUETTE SIGNATURE. A long low horizontal band with a dense comb of identical legs beneath it. The
flattest, longest, most horizontal shape in the game and the only many-legged one.

SCALE. 0.6 m tall at the back and 3.5 m long. The human outline stands beside it; it comes to just
above the human's knee and is twice as long as the human is tall.

LIGHT. Amber, offset backward along the whole body, trailing into empty air behind the tail. The
brightest amber sits behind the animal rather than on it.


───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
IMAGE 4 — LABENS LONGITUDINIS, THE DRIFTING ONE.  THE DOOR OUT OF WORLD 2.
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

BEHAVIOUR. It holds a task perfectly at the start and loses it as it goes. Over a long chain of steps
it drifts: the early work is exact, the middle wanders, and by the end it is confidently doing
something else entirely and has no memory of what it set out to do. The player can now check its answer
and repair it — and the next time they meet it, it has forgotten being repaired. Every fix has to be
made again. **This is the wall of the second world:** you have learned to correct, and nothing you
correct stays corrected. Something that survives the exchange is a third-world component.

BODY. A single, enormously LONG, SEGMENTED body, like an articulated spine or a chain of vertebrae,
running away from the camera into the distance. It has no legs. It lies along the ground in a long
shallow S, and it must read as ONE animal rather than a row of things.

THE NEAR END, closest to the camera, is DENSE, PRECISE AND BEAUTIFULLY MADE: tight regular segments,
crisp edges, exact repetition, obviously the work of something that knew what it was doing.

ALONG ITS LENGTH IT DEGRADES, CONTINUOUSLY AND WITHOUT A BREAK. The segments grow larger, looser and
less regular. The spacing becomes uneven. The alignment wanders off the centre line. Edges soften. By
the far end the segments barely resemble the near ones: bloated, irregular, drifting sideways out of
true, and finally dissolving into the mist without ever reaching a conclusion. There is no damage
anywhere and no break — it is one continuous slide from exactness into nonsense.

It has a HEAD at the near end, small, alert, and looking forward — away from the ruined far end,
entirely unaware of it.

SILHOUETTE SIGNATURE. A long tapering segmented line running into depth, tight and regular at one end
and wide and irregular at the other. The only creature in the game whose silhouette CHANGES ALONG ITS
OWN LENGTH.

SCALE. 0.8 m tall at the near end, and 40 m long running away into the mist. The human outline stands
beside the near end, which reaches the human's hip.

LIGHT. Amber, and it FADES WITH DISTANCE ALONG THE BODY. Bright and warm in the tight near segments,
dimming through the middle, and completely dark in the last third. The light does not fail suddenly. It
runs out, the way the animal's grip on the task runs out.


───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
IMAGE 5 — WORLD SHOT: THE PRICE. What this world feels like to be in.
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

THE SHOT. Mid-wide, camera at standing height, looking across the plain.

THE GROUND IS DIFFERENT HERE. World 1's ground rose into broad, ordered, walkable terraces wherever the
player asked a good question — that terrain is visible far behind, warm and finished. In THIS world the
ground is BARE MARBLED STONE AGAIN, flat and unbuilt, because nothing here is free and nothing here
accumulates by itself. Between the two runs a clean edge.

ACROSS THE BARE STONE, a scatter of small SPENT MARKS: shallow dark craters in the stone, a hand's span
each, where a call has been paid for and used up. Dozens of them, some fresh with a faint amber ember
still cooling in the base, most cold and dead. They make a visible trail behind the probe. The player
has been spending.

TWO CREATURES, well apart:
  · mid-distance left, the tall poised wading bird, fully and beautifully lit in amber, needle beak
    aimed down at an empty patch of stone, absolutely still.
  · near right and low, the long many-legged animal walking slowly, its amber lagging a segment behind
    it and trailing in the air past its tail.

THE PROBE is a single small warm amber point with a fading ribbon, in the near third, and its ribbon is
noticeably SHORTER than it was in the first world. It cannot afford to wander.

MOOD. Sharper and colder than World 1. The first world was generous and indifferent. This one is exact
and expensive. Something here can finally tell you that you are wrong, and it charges you to say so.


───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
IMAGE 6 — WORLD SHOT: THE FORGETTING. The door.
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

THE SHOT. Wide, camera slightly elevated, looking down the length of the drifting creature as it runs
away from the viewer into the mist.

THE CREATURE fills the composition: the long segmented body, tight and bright and exact in the
foreground where the probe has been working on it, degrading continuously along its length, dark and
bloated and wandering by the far end, dissolving into fog without a conclusion.

THE REPAIR IS VISIBLE AND IT IS LOCAL. Around the near segments the amber is strong and the segments
are perfectly aligned — the player has been here and fixed this. A few metres along, the alignment is
already wandering again. The correction did not travel. It stopped where the probe stopped.

THE PROBE hovers over the repaired near section, small and warm, with a short ribbon. It is working.
It will have to do this again.

FAR OFF, at the very limit of visibility through the mist and small enough to be almost missed: A
SECOND CREATURE OF THE SAME KIND, whole along its entire length — tight, regular and bright from end to
end, with no degradation anywhere. It is the only intact one in sight and it is out of reach, on the
far side of everything.

That is all the signal the next world needs. Do not add a gate, a door, a portal, an arch or a
structure of any kind. Nothing is locked. There is simply one creature out there that kept what it
learned.

MOOD. Not despairing. Patient, and slightly Sisyphean, with one distant thing worth walking toward.
