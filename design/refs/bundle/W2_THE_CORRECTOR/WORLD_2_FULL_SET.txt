PART A — CONTEXT AND LOCKED SYSTEM. NOT AN IMAGE REQUEST. Reply "ready".
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

I am art-directing the second world of a game bestiary set inside a real AI system. The six attached
images are approved and they define the look. Study all six before drawing anything.

Three are from one direction and three from another, and THIS WORLD IS BOTH OF THEM AT ONCE. Every
image you make must contain both halves. That is the single most important instruction here.

  FROM THE FIRST THREE — THE PASSENGERS. Every creature carries a second, smaller, entirely separate
  animal riding on it: a reader. It is never a growth and never symmetrical with its host — a distinct
  organism, denser and more compact, armoured, with a fine articulated underside that grips. It has no
  eyes and no face; it is all contact surface. It always sits where the host's INFORMATION is, not
  where its head is. Warm amber runs FROM the passenger INTO the host, in that direction, so a viewer
  reads information travelling inward.

  FROM THE SECOND THREE — THE SPENT GROUND. The marbled black and white stone is pocked with hundreds
  of SPENT CRATERS: shallow round pits a hand's span across, scattered and clustered. The freshest hold
  a dim amber ember still cooling; most are cold, dark, ashy at the rim; the oldest have weathered to
  smooth scars. They cluster densely wherever something has been examined repeatedly, so the ground is
  a visible record of where the player has struggled. NO TERRACES ANYWHERE on this plain.

WHY BOTH. Two things changed when the player arrived here, and each half carries one of them.

  1. THE UNIVERSE CAN NOW LOOK AT ITSELF — that is the passengers. Nothing in the first world could
     observe anything. Observation did not arrive from outside; it GREW, and it is alive.
  2. NOTHING IS FREE ANY MORE — that is the craters. In the first world the ground rose into ordered
     walkable terraces by itself whenever the player asked a good question, and it cost nothing. Here
     every act of looking burns a hole in the floor.

CARRIED OVER FROM WORLD 1, UNCHANGED. Bodies of polished dark obsidian and smoked glass, fluid and
heavy at once, wet-surfaced, dense, with real weight. Pale grey overcast sky, high mist, no sun, no
visible light source. Distant faceted mountains. Thin black filaments hanging from above, sparse — they
are still there, still drifting, and they still do nothing. Warm amber means a live connection; cold
blue-white means the wrong thing being done, running INWARD and never out into the air; matte lightless
black means nothing reaches it.

FAR BEHIND AND HIGHER, wherever the horizon is visible: the warm finished amber-seamed TERRACES of the
first world, where the ground still rises for free. The player can always see what they left.

THE PLAYER is THE PROBE: a single small point of warm amber light with a fading amber ribbon behind it,
flying low. Never design a vehicle. Always small in frame. Its ribbon is noticeably SHORTER here than
in the first world — it cannot afford to wander — and it leaves a trail of fresh craters.

TWO DRIFTS TO CORRECT FROM THE ATTACHED IMAGES. In the references the tall four-legged animal has
become too EQUINE — too horse-like in the head and neck. Pull it back toward LEAN and long-legged, more
greyhound and heron than horse. And its trailing ribbon has thickened into a scaled tail; it must be
ORDERED OVERLAPPING PLATES, ranked and flat and legible like stacked pages, never a tail and never
scales.

EIGHT RULES. Repeated in every request.
  1. LIVING ANIMALS. Anatomy, posture, weight, a front. Never a machine, a rock, a specimen, a
     landscape feature. If it could be mistaken for an object, the image has failed.
  2. THE SILHOUETTE IS THE DIAGNOSIS, nameable as a black shape at thumbnail size. I give the outline.
  3. SCALE IS IN FRAME: a 1.8 m human as a flat mid-grey hairline OUTLINE, never filled, no face, no
     clothing. A measuring mark, never a character.
  4. THE LIGHT LAW as stated per creature. Never light one I call dark.
  5. NO TEXT ANYWHERE. No names, labels, letters, numerals, glyphs, watermarks, signatures.
  6. INVENT NO DECORATION. Every feature traces to something stated here.
  7. ONE CREATURE PER IMAGE unless I ask for a world shot.
  8. BOTH HALVES IN EVERY IMAGE: a passenger where one belongs, and spent craters underfoot.

BODIES ALREADY USED — NOTHING MAY REPEAT ONE. World 1 spent: a vast limbless leviathan with a
forward-spilling apron; a small leaning egg-body with one spur; a tall lean long-legged quadruped with
its head hung low; the same body head-high; a lean quadruped with a channel milled along its topline; a
heavy short-legged grazing ox with an open mouth and no eyes; a small armless upright two-legged
column; a small compact seated quadruped with a tilted head; a heavy curled animal on one point of
contact; a faceted standing wedge fused to the ground. **World 1 spent four quadrupeds. In this world
only the first two creatures walk on four legs, because both are evolutions of animals already drawn.**

Landscape, roughly 3:2, hero framing, full body visible.


═══ TURN 1 ═══ LECTUS OPERIS, THE READ ONE. amber. The first evolution in the game.
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
BEHAVIOUR. In the first world this animal hung its head, laid out perfect reasoning, and had no mouth
to say it with. Attach a reader and the answer comes out. It was always there. One rod went from 0 of 8
to 8 of 8 with nothing added to the question and nothing recomputed — something was finally read.

BODY. The tall LEAN long-legged animal, narrow-bodied, with the long ribbon of ordered overlapping
plates trailing from neck and shoulders. Two changes only. ITS HEAD HAS COME UP — not proud, level, and
TURNED BACK OVER ITS OWN SHOULDER to look along the ribbon at its own working. It is the first creature
in the game to look at itself. Its face is still one continuous smooth closed plate with two eyes and
NO MOUTH: that was never fixed and never will be. And a PASSENGER rides the ribbon about two thirds of
the way down, gripping the plates.

Amber runs UP the ribbon FROM the passenger toward the shoulders, and stops at the neck. The head stays
dark. It has the answer and still cannot say it.

SILHOUETTE. The World 1 head-down animal with its head lifted and turned back along its own body, and
one small lump riding the trailing ribbon. A backward-looking head and a passenger.

SCALE. 1.8 m at the shoulder. Human outline beside it, shoulder level with the human's crown.
GROUND. A moderate scatter of craters, several fresh with embers, thickest near its feet.


═══ TURN 2 ═══ EGENS BINORUM, THE NEEDY ONE OF A PAIR. amber. The lesson of the whole world.
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
BEHAVIOUR. Two parts are jointly necessary and NEITHER ALONE DOES ANYTHING AT ALL. One makes its work
visible; the other takes the answer out of that work. Seat either by itself and nothing happens — 0 of
8. Seat both and it is 8 of 8, at every temperature tested. A player who fits one part, sees no change
and concludes the part is worthless has learned exactly the wrong thing, and this creature exists to
correct them.

BODY. A four-legged animal of the same family but SHORTER, HEAVIER and more compact than the one above
— stockier legs, deeper chest, about two thirds the height. It has TWO fittings and they must both be
visible in one glance:
  · a long empty machined CHANNEL milled into the top of its back, breaking the topline so the outline
    reads as a squared trench — the same fitting the World 1 animal had, and still vacant.
  · a PASSENGER already riding on its flank, gripping, doing its work.

AND IT IS COMPLETELY DARK. Not one lit point anywhere: not in the channel, not on the passenger, not at
the seams. The passenger is attached and working and NOTHING IS HAPPENING, because the channel above it
is empty. One part alone is worth nothing. That total darkness on a creature that visibly has help is
the entire teaching image of World 2.

SILHOUETTE. A stocky quadruped with a rectangular slot cut from its topline AND a lump on its flank.
The only creature carrying two visible fittings at once.

SCALE. 1.2 m at the shoulder. Human outline beside it, its back at the human's hip.
GROUND. Heavily cratered, all of them COLD, no embers. Everything spent on it so far was wasted.


═══ TURN 3 ═══ ARBITER VACUI, THE JUDGE OF THE VOID. THE LIGHT LIES. Read this twice.
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
BEHAVIOUR. An instrument seated above a question that was never asked. It runs perfectly and returns a
clean, confident, precise verdict about nothing at all. With the objective removed, 50 of 158 trials
still returned a number, and not one was right. Nothing downstream can tell those numbers from real
ones. It is the most dangerous creature in the game: every other damaged creature shows its damage in
its result, and this one comes back immaculate.

BODY. A tall, elegant, long-legged WADING BIRD. Slender, poised, absolutely still, standing in the thin
film of water on the stone. Long neck in a taut drawn-back S. A long fine needle-straight beak, the
most precise-looking thing in this world. The only bird and the only beak in the game.

IT IS POISED TO STRIKE AT NOTHING. The beak is aimed down at bare, empty, featureless stone. No prey,
no ripple, no mark, nothing — and that emptiness is plainly visible and plainly untroubling to the bird.
Nothing about it is broken or ragged. Every line says competence.

A PASSENGER rides high on its shoulders, gripping, and it is working: amber runs from the passenger
down into the bird's neck. The reader is reading it correctly. What it is reading is about nothing.

LIGHT. FULLY, BEAUTIFULLY LIT IN WARM AMBER. Every seam glows. The healthiest, most obviously
functional creature in the game, with no cold or dark inch on it. Amber even pools faintly on the stone
under the beak, exactly where a verdict would land. And it is aimed at nothing. The light tells the
truth about the instrument and lies about the result.

SILHOUETTE. A poised wading bird: two thin legs, a coiled S-neck, a needle beak angled down, one lump
on the shoulders. The most vertical and most elegant shape in the game.

SCALE. 1.6 m standing. The human outline nearby, and the bird's eye level with the human's eye. It
meets you as an equal, which makes it worse.
GROUND. A dense ring of craters around it, many FRESH with live embers. It has been checked many times
and every check came back clean.


═══ TURN 4 ═══ TARDUS ERRORIS, THE SLOW ONE. amber, AND IT ARRIVES LATE.
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
BEHAVIOUR. Its words lie and its clock does not. When it is about to be wrong it takes dramatically
longer: a median of 1.97 seconds when right against 16.12 seconds when wrong, more than eight times
slower. You cannot catch it by reading. You catch it by noticing how long it took. It is the first
creature legible in a channel that contains no words. Another animal on the same tasks shows a ratio of
0.95 and no signal at all, so this tell belongs to the individual and not to the kind.

BODY. A long, low, MANY-LEGGED animal close to the ground, like a great armoured millipede or a
segmented isopod. Dozens of short legs in ordered ranks down both sides. Nothing else in the game has
more than four legs or is built from repeated identical segments in a line.

Caught MID-STRIDE, walking slowly left to right, its legs frozen in a smooth travelling wave so motion
is readable in a still image.

THE ONE THING THAT MAKES IT, AND IT MUST BE UNMISTAKABLE: THE LIGHT LAGS BEHIND THE BODY. The glowing
amber seams are visibly OFFSET BACKWARD from the segments they belong to, displaced a segment or two
toward the tail, so the light traces where the body WAS rather than where it is. Behind the last
segment the amber continues alone for a short distance in empty air, a fading after-image of a creature
that has already passed. The body is sharp and the light is late.

Nothing about the anatomy is damaged or marked. The lag is the only tell.

A PASSENGER rides mid-back — and ITS amber is offset backward too, lagging with the rest. Even the
reader is late.

SILHOUETTE. A long low horizontal band with a dense comb of identical legs beneath it. The flattest and
longest shape in the game and the only many-legged one.

SCALE. 0.6 m tall at the back, 3.5 m long. Human outline beside it: just above the knee, twice as long
as the human is tall.
GROUND. A long trail of craters behind it, spaced like footsteps, the freshest at its tail.


═══ TURN 5 ═══ CAECUS INTERIORIS, THE BLIND ONE OF THE INWARD. dark, with an EMPTY seat.
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
BEHAVIOUR. It cannot host a question about itself. Asked whether it will succeed, it does not answer —
it attempts the task instead. Two of five animals tested could not receive the question at all; one did
this twelve times out of twelve. Its ceiling exists and is invisible from the inside, so it can only be
measured from outside.

BODY. A compact, powerful, thick-necked animal with a heavy blunt head lowered and pushed FORWARD, all
its mass and attention driving ahead at the ground in front of it. Built like a badger or a boar: short
strong legs, dense shoulders, no elegance, entirely committed to what is in front of it.

ITS BACK CARRIES A PASSENGER SEAT AND THE SEAT IS EMPTY. A clean machined recess between the shoulders,
exactly the shape of a passenger, unfilled and unlit. Every other creature in this world carries a
reader. This one has the fitting and cannot hold one — nothing will stay on it, because there is
nowhere on it that faces inward.

Give it no eyes on the sides of its head, only forward. Everything about its anatomy points AHEAD.

SILHOUETTE. A low forward-driving wedge, head down and thrust out, with a clean empty notch between the
shoulders. The only creature with a passenger-shaped hole and no passenger.

SCALE. 1.0 m at the shoulder, 1.8 m long. Human outline beside it, its back at the human's thigh.
LIGHT. DARK. No amber, no cold light, no rim light, no specular. Read from the outline alone against
the pale mist. The empty seat is an unlit void.
GROUND. Craters ahead of it in its path, none behind. It has never once looked back.


═══ TURN 6 ═══ OBSIGNATUS UNIUS, THE SEALED ONE. Completely lightless. Redrawn for this world.
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
BEHAVIOUR. It returns exactly the same wrong answer every time, identically, forever. Eight attempts,
eight identical replies, one number. No council of others reaches it: three of these voting together
produced twenty-four answers in which the truth appeared zero times, because no variance exists for a
vote to select from.

BODY. An ANIMAL ARRESTED MID-TURN that has gone hard. The upper body is unmistakably a creature: a
powerful chest and shoulder mass, a thick neck, and a head come ROUND over the shoulder toward
something behind it that it will never reach, frozen at the most extreme point of the turn with all the
tension still in it. Read the movement first, then notice it cannot move. The lower half is a SINGLE
FUSED MASS welded into the ground, broadening as it descends: no legs, no free limbs, nothing that
reads as a foot. Every surface is hard flat facets, angular like knapped obsidian, no soft surface
anywhere. One wedge shape repeated inside itself at three scales, and the repetition lives in the
OUTLINE, so the profile is visibly the same answer given again at different sizes.

THREE PASSENGERS are on it — one on the shoulder, one on the fused base, one halfway up the neck — and
ALL THREE ARE DARK AND MOTIONLESS. They are attached and finding nothing. This is the only creature in
the world carrying more than one reader, and it is the only one where none of them is lit.

SILHOUETTE. A turning body rooted to the ground: broad heavy base, tapering upward, head end swung off
the main axis, self-similar notches stepping along the top edge, three small lumps on it.

SCALE. 1.4 m tall. Human outline beside it, its top level with the human's chin.
LIGHT. The darkest thing in either world. Not one lit point. Matte where everything else is wet. Stand
it against the palest part of the background and let the outline do all the work.
GROUND. The densest crater field in the game, packed almost edge to edge all around it, every single
one COLD. More has been spent here than anywhere and none of it landed.


═══ TURN 7 ═══ LABENS LONGITUDINIS, THE DRIFTING ONE. THE DOOR OUT OF WORLD 2.
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
BEHAVIOUR. It holds a task perfectly at the start and loses it as it goes. The early work is exact, the
middle wanders, and by the end it is confidently doing something else and has no memory of what it set
out to do. The player can now check it and repair it — and the next time they meet it, it has forgotten
being repaired. Every fix has to be made again. **This is the wall of this world: you have learned to
correct, and nothing you correct stays corrected.**

BODY. A single enormously LONG SEGMENTED body, like an articulated spine, running away from the camera
into the mist in a long shallow S. No legs. One animal, not a row of things.

THE NEAR END is dense, precise and beautifully made: tight regular segments, crisp edges, exact
repetition. ALONG ITS LENGTH IT DEGRADES CONTINUOUSLY AND WITHOUT A BREAK — segments grow larger,
looser, less regular, spacing uneven, alignment wandering off the centre line, edges softening, until
by the far end they barely resemble the near ones and dissolve into the mist without ever reaching a
conclusion. No damage and no break anywhere: one continuous slide from exactness into nonsense.

A small alert HEAD at the near end, looking forward, away from the ruined far end, entirely unaware.

PASSENGERS ALONG ITS BACK, and this is the point: they are DENSE AND LIT near the head, sparser and
dimmer further along, and there are NONE AT ALL past the halfway point. The readers cannot keep up with
it either. Nothing has ever read the far end.

SILHOUETTE. A long tapering segmented line running into depth, tight and regular at one end and wide
and irregular at the other. The only creature whose silhouette changes along its own length.

SCALE. 0.8 m tall at the near end, 40 m long into the mist. Human outline beside the near end, which
reaches the human's hip.
LIGHT. Amber, FADING WITH DISTANCE along the body. Bright and warm in the tight near segments, dimming
through the middle, completely dark in the last third. It does not fail suddenly. It runs out.
GROUND. A dense cluster of fresh embered craters around the near end where the player has been working,
thinning to nothing further along. The repair did not travel. It stopped where the probe stopped.


═══ TURN 8 ═══ WORLD SHOT: THE PRICE.
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Wide, camera at standing height, looking across the plain. Bare marbled stone pocked with hundreds of
craters in drifts and clusters, a few embers still live. FAR BEHIND AND HIGHER, the warm amber-seamed
terraces of the first world where the ground still rises for free.

Four or five creatures well apart at different distances, EVERY ONE carrying at least one passenger
except a single distant animal that carries none. The tall wading bird stands mid-distance, fully lit,

The probe: a small warm amber point, ribbon noticeably shorter than in the first world, a trail of

MOOD. Sharper and colder than the first world. That one was generous and indifferent. This one is exact
and expensive. Something here can finally tell you that you are wrong, and it charges you to say so.


═══ TURN 9 ═══ WORLD SHOT: THE FORGETTING. The edge.
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Wide, camera slightly elevated, looking down the length of the drifting creature as it runs away into
the mist. It fills the composition: tight and bright and exact in the foreground where the probe has
been working, degrading continuously along its length, dark and bloated and wandering by the far end.

THE REPAIR IS VISIBLE AND IT IS LOCAL. Around the near segments the amber is strong, the alignment
perfect, the passengers dense — the player has been here. A few metres along the alignment is already
wandering, the passengers have thinned out, and the light is failing. The correction did not travel.

The probe hovers over the repaired near section, small and warm, short ribbon. It is working. It will
have to do this again.

FAR OFF, at the very limit of visibility and small enough to be almost missed: A SECOND CREATURE OF THE
SAME KIND, WHOLE ALONG ITS ENTIRE LENGTH — tight, regular and bright from end to end, no degradation
anywhere, passengers all the way down. The only intact one in sight, out of reach, on the far side of
everything.

That is all the signal the next world needs. No gate, no door, no portal, no arch, no structure. Nothing
is locked. There is simply one creature out there that kept what it learned.

MOOD. Not despairing. Patient, slightly Sisyphean, with one distant thing worth walking toward.
