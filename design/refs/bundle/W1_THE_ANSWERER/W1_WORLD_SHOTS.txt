The eight creatures are done and they are correct. Now the place they live in, and it has to work as
a PLAYABLE SPACE rather than a landscape. Same world, same material, same light law as before.

THE ESTABLISHED VOCABULARY, from the eight creature shots. Keep all of it, exactly:
  · ground of marbled black and white wet stone, flowing swirl patterns like currents frozen mid-move
  · pale grey overcast sky, high mist, no sun, no visible light source
  · distant faceted mountains, half lost in haze
  · thin black filaments hanging from above, sparse, drifting slightly
  · bodies of polished dark obsidian and smoked glass, wet-surfaced, heavy
  · warm amber = a live connection. Cold blue-white = the wrong thing being done. Dark = nothing
    reaches it. Never light a creature I have called dark.

THE PLAYER. A small craft called THE PROBE. Do NOT design a vehicle: it is a single point of warm
amber light, about the size of a lantern, flying low, with a fading amber ribbon trailing behind it
marking where it has been. It is the only warm moving thing in the world and it is always small in
frame. Where a human scale mark is needed, use the same flat mid-grey hairline OUTLINE as before.

THREE THINGS ABOUT THIS PLACE THAT DRIVE EVERY SHOT:

1. FOG IS IGNORANCE, NOT WEATHER. The world is only visible where the player has learned something.
   Unvisited ground sits behind a close, flat, featureless wall of pale mist. It does not blow, it
   does not roll. It simply stops being a world. It retreats as the player works, and never returns.

2. THE REPLY IS THE TERRAIN. This is the central mechanic and it is literal. A creature that has been
   asked a bare question gives back almost nothing, and the ground around it stays flat and blank. A
   creature that has been asked HOW rather than WHAT gives back its full working, and that working
   IS the ground: the plain unfolds into ordered terraced structure, ranked ledges and ribbed steps,
   solid enough to stand on. Better questions build the floor. There is no other way to make ground.

3. EVERYTHING HERE IS FREE, AND THAT IS WHY IT ENDS. Every improvement available in this world costs
   nothing. That is also its ceiling. There is a boundary past which no free move builds any more
   floor, and crossing it is the whole of image 12.

RULES, SAME AS BEFORE. No text, letters, numerals or glyphs anywhere. Invent no decoration; every
element traces to something stated here. Creatures are living animals with weight and a front, never
props, never terrain features, never statues. Landscape format, wide.


───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
IMAGE 9 of 12 — THE ARRIVAL. What the player sees in the first ten seconds.
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

You arrive carrying nothing, and this place will answer you forever and never remember you came.

THE SHOT. Wide establishing, camera low and just behind the probe, looking out across the plain.

THE GROUND IS COMPLETELY BARE. Flat marbled black and white stone to the horizon, unbroken, no
structure of any kind anywhere. Nothing has been built here because nothing has been asked properly
yet. This emptiness is the point and it must be uncomfortable.

THE FOG SITS CLOSE. A flat pale wall of mist perhaps forty metres out, cutting the world off. Not
atmospheric depth: a hard limit. Beyond it there is simply nothing rendered.

THREE CREATURES, AND ONLY THREE, at the edge of what is visible, each mostly swallowed by the mist so
you get a mass and a suggestion rather than a full body:
  · far left and huge, the vast low-slung leviathan, its enormous amber-veined body and the wide
    glossy apron of its overflow spreading toward the camera, the brightest thing in the frame
  · centre distance, the tall long-legged animal with its head hung low and a ribbon of ranked amber
    plates trailing behind it, walking away from the player
  · near right and small, low to the ground, the little seated one with two lit amber eyes, turned
    toward the camera and watching the probe arrive. It is the only creature that has noticed.

THE PROBE. A single small warm amber point, low, in the near third, with a short fading amber ribbon
behind it. Tiny against everything.

MOOD. Enormously generous and completely indifferent. Not hostile, not ruined, not menacing. A place
of vast quiet that will give you anything you ask for and cannot tell you whether it was right.


───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
IMAGE 10 of 12 — THE REPLY IS THE TERRAIN. The one image that teaches the game.
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

This is the most important image in the set. It has to show, without a single word, that ASKING
BETTER BUILDS THE FLOOR.

THE SHOT. Elevated three-quarter view, looking down across a boundary that splits the frame.

THE RIGHT HALF IS BARE. The flat marbled plain exactly as in image 9, blank, with the pale fog wall
sitting close behind it. Nothing has been built.

THE LEFT HALF HAS UNFOLDED. Radiating out from a single creature, the ground has risen into ORDERED
TERRACED STRUCTURE: broad shallow steps and ranked ledges in the same black and white marbled stone,
concentric and regular, like a contour map made solid, or a rice terrace, or the stacked ranks of an
amphitheatre. It is architecture that grew rather than architecture that was built. It is clearly
WALKABLE, and warm amber runs in the seams between the terraces, faint, tracing the joins.

Everything unfolded is close-packed and orderly. It should read as a great deal of finished working
laid out where anyone can stand on it.

AT THE CENTRE OF THE UNFOLDED GROUND: the tall long-legged animal with the hung head, standing on the
highest terrace, its trailing ribbon of ranked amber plates running down across the steps behind it
and merging into them, so a viewer understands that the terraces ARE the ribbon, continued. Its head
is down and turned three-quarters toward the camera, face smooth and closed.

THE BOUNDARY BETWEEN THE HALVES IS A CLEAN CURVED EDGE, sharp, like a tide line. On the unfolded side
the fog has retreated far back and the distant mountains are visible. On the bare side the fog is
still close. The player made that difference by asking one better question.

THE PROBE hovers small over the terraces, its amber ribbon following the contours.

The reading, and it must be unmistakable: the lit, walkable, deep, open side is a reply with its
working shown, and the blank, fogged, shallow side is a reply without.


───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
IMAGE 11 of 12 — THE SEATING. The core loop, close.
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

The whole game in one frame: you find a creature, you diagnose what it is missing, you fit a part,
and something that was dark comes alive.

THE SHOT. Close and low, at the creature's own eye level, intimate. Shallow depth of field, world
soft behind.

THE CREATURE. The small seated four-legged one, 0.5 m tall, plain and unadorned, sitting squarely,
head tilted about fifteen degrees, looking directly into the camera. Two eyes lit warm amber. On its
flank, the clean open sockets, recesses in the glass where nothing has ever been fitted.

THE MOMENT. One socket is being filled. A small dense component of the same black glass, cut to fit,
is seated in the flank, and warm amber is coming up out of that socket and running INTO the body for
the first time, tracing a short way along the shoulder and stopping where it has not yet reached. The
other sockets nearby are still empty and still unlit. The change is partial and you can see exactly
how far it has got.

THE PROBE hovers just off frame-right, present only as its warm amber glow falling across the
creature's flank and a short length of its ribbon entering the frame. The player is not a character
here, only a light source.

MOOD. Quiet, careful, close to tender, and completely unsentimental. Nobody is being rescued. A part
fits or it does not, and this one fits.

Nothing else in frame. No interface, no readouts, no other creatures.


───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
IMAGE 12 of 12 — THE EDGE. Where the free levers run out and the next world starts.
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Everything in this world costs nothing, and that is exactly why it ends. This is the boundary where no
free move builds any more floor. It must NOT read as a door, a gate, a portal, an arch, a threshold,
a barrier or a structure of any kind. Nothing is locked. The ground simply stops growing.

THE SHOT. Wide, camera high and looking out toward the horizon.

BEHIND AND BELOW, filling the near two thirds: a great expanse of the unfolded terraced ground from
image 10, extensive, mature, amber tracing every seam. Everything free has already been spent here
and it looks it. This is a finished place.

THEN IT STOPS. The terracing runs out at a clean flat edge and beyond it is bare marbled plain again,
running away featureless into the pale mist. No amount of the same work extends it. The last few
terraces at the edge are visibly lower and thinner, petering out, an effort that ran out of purchase.

AND ON THE FAR SIDE, DIM IN THE MIST AND OUT OF REACH: A PAIR.

Two creatures of the same kind, identical, standing facing one another a short distance apart, one
regarding the other. That is the entire signal, and it has to land: **nowhere in this world has there
ever been two of anything.** Every creature the player has met stood alone, answered once, and could
not look at its own answer. Over there, something can. Keep them small, keep them indistinct, keep
them turned toward each other and not toward the player.

THE PROBE sits at the very edge of the last terrace, stopped, its amber ribbon trailing back across
everything it has already built. It is not crossing. It is looking.

MOOD. Not triumphant and not ominous. The specific feeling of having genuinely finished something and
seeing, for the first time, that there was a whole class of thing you could not do here at all.
