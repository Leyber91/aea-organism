# SCENE 13 · THE MAP — an OPTIONS BRIEF, not a render spec

*Paste `00_CANON_BLOCK.md` first (the visual law), then this. **Do not ask for one finished map.** Ask
for STRUCTURAL PROPOSALS — several distinct shapes that could hold all of this — one image per message.*

---

## THE ASK

I need to represent an entire architecture as a single readable map. **I do not know what shape it
should be.** Below is everything that must fit and every constraint the shape must satisfy. Propose a
STRUCTURE. Then propose a different one. I want options, with the trade-off of each named — not one
answer.

Draw each proposal as a **technical diagram of the layout itself**: the topology, the regions, where
each family of elements sits, and how the eye travels. Label regions, not individual items. Treat it as
an information-architecture sketch that happens to be beautiful, not as finished game art.

---

## WHAT MUST FIT — the complete inventory, 86 items in ten families

The hard part is that the families have wildly different sizes. A shape that handles 3 gracefully often
handles 28 terribly. That is the real design problem.

| # | family | count | note |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **THE QUESTION** | 1 | the root: *"what must exist for indefinite growth?"* Everything answers it |
| 2 | **AXES** | 5 | PATH · ABSTRACTION · MULTIPLICITY · PROMPTING · ASYNC |
| 3 | **LADDER RUNGS** | 30 | each axis has six levels, L0–L5. **This is the progress readout** |
| 4 | **SEEDS** | 10 | 6 fire constantly, 4 fire only on a condition — the split must be visible |
| 5 | **MECHANICS** | 4 | one of the four never acts; it only recognises exhaustion and triggers others |
| 6 | **OPERATIONS** | 4 | **these are not places — they are things that HAPPEN TO the map.** Transitions, not nodes |
| 7 | **PRINCIPLES** | 3 | overlay the whole thing rather than sitting anywhere |
| 8 | **THE INNOVATION LAYER** | 28 | one cycle, 8 trigger conditions, 5 filters, 7 proposal types, 1 registry, 6 tools. **The deepest and most numerous family, and currently entirely unbuilt** |
| 9 | **ENGINEERING LAYERS** | 11 | recovery, self-measurement, resource management |
| 10 | **RUNTIME FABRIC** | 9 | the tick order, the shared state, the observable record |

There is also an **11-layer dependency stack** running through all of it: layer 0 is the question,
layer 10 is the finished thing, and **each layer requires every layer beneath it — remove one and
everything above stops.** Any shape that cannot express that ordering is disqualified.

---

## THE SEVEN CONSTRAINTS ANY SHAPE MUST SATISFY

1. **PROGRESSIVE LIGHTING.** The player completes a chapter and a region lights up. The shape needs
   clearly separable regions that can go from dark to lit, one at a time, ten times.
2. **DEPENDENCY MUST BE LEGIBLE.** Something must make it obvious that upper things rest on lower things.
3. **CARDINALITY MISMATCH.** It has to hold a family of 3 and a family of 28 without the 28 swamping it.
4. **THE 30 RUNGS ARE THE SCOREBOARD.** They change most often and must be readable at a glance —
   this is where the player looks to see how far they have come.
5. **LEGIBLE AT A GLANCE, DEEP ON DEMAND.** Roughly 30 marks maximum on the surface. Anything more
   numerous must live one level down, revealed when asked for.
6. **THE UNBUILT MUST BE PRESENT BUT DISTINCT.** Two-thirds of this is not made yet. It must be
   visible as named-and-absent, never hidden and never faked as complete.
7. **TWO INKS.** Per the canon block: near-black, warm grey, and a tiny amount of earned amber.

## STRUCTURAL FAMILIES TO CONSIDER — start here, and go past them

Do not feel bound to these. They are provided so the proposals are genuinely different from each other
rather than variations on one idea.

- **CONCENTRIC** — rings around a core. Natural for dependency-as-depth. Risk: outer rings get huge, and
  a fifth ring breaks legibility.
- **STRATA / SECTION** — a vertical cutaway, layer 0 at the bottom, layer 10 at the top. Literally
  matches the dependency stack. Risk: hard to fit 28 of anything in a band.
- **SPIRAL** — one continuous path from centre to edge. Matches the fact that the game IS a walk, and
  progress is distance travelled. Risk: no room for parallel families.
- **CIRCUIT / TRANSIT MAP** — nodes on lines, interchanges where families meet. Excellent for
  dependency. Risk: reads as a diagram rather than a place.
- **PERIODIC TABLE** — a grid where position encodes properties. Handles large families well and is
  scannable. Risk: static, unatmospheric, no sense of journey.
- **CUTAWAY OF A MACHINE** — a reactor section, chambers of different sizes. Fits the world exactly
  (the world is a thinking power installation). Risk: irregular shapes are hard to light cleanly.
- **NESTED CONTAINMENT** — regions inside regions inside regions. Good for depth-on-demand. Risk:
  everything looks like boxes.
- **CONSTELLATION with named territories** — regions of a dark field, some charted, some not. Good for
  the unbuilt. Risk: implies space, which this world is not.

## HOW TO ANSWER

For each proposal, in one image:
1. Draw the topology clearly, with regions labelled by family name and count (e.g. `SEEDS · 10`).
2. Show where the 30 ladder rungs live and how they read as a scoreboard.
3. Show how the 28-item family is handled without swamping the surface.
4. Mark one region as **lit** and the rest as dark, so the lighting mechanism is visible.
5. Indicate where the unbuilt two-thirds sits and how it is distinguished.
6. Underneath, in engraved caps, one line: what this shape is GOOD at, and one line: what it COSTS.

**Then stop, and I will ask for the next proposal.** One shape per image. I am choosing between them,
not collecting them.
