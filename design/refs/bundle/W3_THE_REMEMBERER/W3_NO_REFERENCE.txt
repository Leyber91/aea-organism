I am designing the third region of a game set inside a real AI system. You have not seen the first
two and you do not need to. Design this one from what it IS.

## THE PREMISE, AND IT IS NOT WHAT YOU WILL FIRST ASSUME

This place **keeps everything that has ever happened to it, and cannot find any of it.**

Not a place that forgets. Not a ruin, not a graveyard, not an archive, not a library. **Nothing here
is ever lost — that is precisely the problem.** Every exchange, every answer, every correction is
still present, still intact, still exactly where it was put. And retrieval is a completely separate
capability from storage, one this world does not have. It is drowning in its own perfectly preserved
past.

**Design the place from that.** What does a world look like where nothing decays, nothing is discarded,
nothing is overwritten, and nothing can be found again? The failure mode here is not decay. **It is
abundance without an index.**

## FOUR THINGS IT MUST NOT BE, BECAUSE THEY ARE WHAT EVERYONE REACHES FOR

Say what you are avoiding before you design.

1. **NOT STRATA.** No sediment, no geological layers, no varves, no rings, no cross-sections. Time as
   depth is the obvious answer and it is taken.
2. **NOT TERRACES.** No stepped platforms, no ranked ledges, no rice-terrace or travertine forms.
3. **NOT AN ARCHIVE.** No shelves, no vaults, no filing, no card indexes, no libraries, no ruins of
   libraries. The metaphor is too literal and it kills the image.
4. **NOT A GREY OVERCAST PLAIN.** Give it a light source, or several, or a strange one.

## THE CREATURES — three animals, and each is a measured behaviour

These are real behaviours from a real system. Each body must BE its behaviour, readable as a black
silhouette at thumbnail size, and **no two may share a body plan.** Give each one a real-world
anatomical referent — a specific animal, not a shape.

**THE ONE THAT HOLDS.** A long segmented animal, and its whole length is exact: every segment tight,
regular, identically spaced, end to end, however far it runs. It is the only creature here that keeps
a thing correctly for as long as you like. Nothing about it is remarkable except that it does not
drift, and in this world that is extraordinary. It is very long — long enough that its far end is a
different part of the landscape.

**THE ONE THAT CARRIES A MISTAKE PERFECTLY.** The same faithfulness, aimed at the wrong thing. Give it
something it should never have kept and it will preserve that flawlessly, forever, with the same care.
It is not broken and it is not lying. **It is indifferent to what it carries**, and that indifference
is the whole creature. Its body should say *this is being held with enormous care* while what it holds
is visibly wrong.

**THE ONE THAT CANNOT FIND.** The centre of this world. It has kept **everything** — more than any
other creature, immaculately — and it cannot locate a single item of it. Its body should be
overwhelmingly full and completely unsearchable: too much, all present, all inaccessible. It is not
distressed. It does not know anything is missing, because nothing IS missing. It simply cannot reach
in.

## THE LIGHT LAW — the only thing carried over, and it is not decoration

A player diagnoses a creature by colour before they read its shape. Three states, and exactly one
applies per creature:

- **WARM AMBER** — a live connection. Something is working. Traced under the surface, at the seams.
- **COLD BLUE-WHITE** — the wrong thing is being done. The same tracery, but running INWARD toward the
  creature's own centre, never outward into the air.
- **MATTE LIGHTLESS BLACK** — nothing reaches it. A light-absorbing surface with no rim light, no edge
  light, no specular hit, no glow. Read only as an outline against the palest part of the background.

`THE ONE THAT HOLDS` is amber. `THE ONE THAT CARRIES A MISTAKE` is amber too — that is the point, it
looks healthy. `THE ONE THAT CANNOT FIND` is amber everywhere and unreachable, which is the hardest
image in the set.

## RULES

- Every creature is a **LIVING ANIMAL** — anatomy, posture, weight, and a front it faces with. Not a
  machine, not a diagram, not a rock, not a plant, not architecture. If it could be mistaken for an
  object, it is wrong.
- **THE SILHOUETTE IS THE DIAGNOSIS.** Nameable in solid black at thumbnail size.
- **A 1.8 m human** appears in every image as a flat mid-grey hairline OUTLINE — never filled, no face,
  no clothing. A measuring mark, never a character.
- **NO TEXT ANYWHERE.** No letters, numerals, glyphs, captions, watermarks, signatures.
- **INVENT NO DECORATION.** Every element traces to something stated here.
- Landscape, roughly 3:2. One creature per creature-image, full body, air around it.

**Then three images:**

2. **THE ONE THAT HOLDS.** Full body, at length, in that place.
3. **THE ONE THAT CANNOT FIND.** Full body. The hardest of the three: full to overflowing, immaculate,
