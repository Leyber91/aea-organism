THE FRAME FOR ALL THREE, AND IT IS THE WHOLE INSTRUCTION:

**THIS IS A THIRD-STAGE EVOLUTION, NOT A NEW WORLD AND NOT A NEW SPECIES.**

Think of how a creature line escalates across three stages. Stage one to stage two is a modest step —
a little larger, one new feature. **Stage two to stage three is enormous**: the same animal, still
instantly recognisable as itself, but massively bigger, armoured, elaborated, crowned, carrying far
more, commanding the space around it. Nobody mistakes a third stage for a different creature. Nobody
mistakes it for the second stage either.

**The attached images are stage two. Make stage three.**

And the same escalation applies to the place. The first two worlds were a flat plain. **A plain is not
a level.** This one has to be somewhere a player can actually go.

I am art-directing the third region of a game set inside a real AI system. The attached images are the
second region. Study them: they are the STAGE TWO of everything you are about to draw.

**THE PLAYER.** A small craft called THE PROBE — a single point of warm amber light with a fading
amber ribbon behind it, flying low. Never a designed vehicle, always small in frame. **The player
flies through this world.** That is not decoration, it is the constraint that decides whether the
design works.

**THIS MUST BE A PLAYABLE SPACE.** Not a landscape, not a diorama, not a concept. Somewhere with:
  · places to **go** — routes, ledges, gaps, openings, things at different heights
  · **verticality** — the first two worlds were flat and it cost them. This one has up and down
  · **landmarks** — things visible from far off that tell a player where they are
  · **scale you can read** — near, middle and far, so flying feels like travelling
If a viewer cannot point at where they would fly, the image has failed however beautiful it is.

**THE FICTION.** In the first region the player could only SPECIFY — say what they wanted and how to
do it. Everything was free. In the second, the world learned to LOOK AT ITSELF: passengers grew on
every creature and read them, and every act of looking burned a hole in the floor. **This third region
KEEPS. What happens here survives.** Nothing is undone and nothing is lost — and the world has no way
to search what it holds. It is not decaying. **It is full.**

**THE LINEAGE — stage two into stage three.** These are the same animals. Do not redesign them.
Escalate them.

  **THE LONG ONE** (attached image 1). Stage two: a segmented body running away into the mist,
  degrading along its length — looser, dimmer, coming apart, never reaching a conclusion.
  **STAGE THREE: it is whole, and it is enormous.** Every segment exact end to end, armoured,
  plated, far heavier and far longer, its passengers no longer scattered but ranked along it in
  order like a spine of them. It does not lie on the landscape — **it is part of the landscape's
  structure.** A player should be able to fly along it.

  **THE BACKWARD-LOOKING ONE** (attached image 2). Stage two: a tall lean four-legged animal, head
  turned back over its shoulder, looking along a trailing ribbon of ordered overlapping plates, face
  a smooth closed plate with two eyes and NO MOUTH, one passenger on the ribbon, amber running up it
  and stopping at the neck. **STAGE THREE: the same animal, far larger and heavier, the ribbon now a
  vast trailing mantle of ranked plates**, many passengers working along it, and the amber now
  reaching past the neck into the head for the first time. Still no mouth. It has the answer, it can
  now see where the answer came from, and it still cannot say it.

**THE LIGHT LAW — unchanged, and it is how a player diagnoses at a glance.**
  · **WARM AMBER** — a live connection, something working. Under the surface, at the seams.
  · **COLD BLUE-WHITE** — the wrong thing being done, running INWARD, never out into the air.
  · **MATTE LIGHTLESS BLACK** — nothing reaches it. No rim light, no specular, read as outline only.

**RULES.** Every creature is a LIVING ANIMAL with anatomy, posture, weight and a front — never a
machine, a rock or architecture. Silhouette is the diagnosis, nameable in black at thumbnail size. A
1.8 m human appears as a flat mid-grey hairline OUTLINE, never filled — a measuring mark, never a
character. No text anywhere. Invent no decoration. Landscape, roughly 3:2.

**AND FIVE THINGS THIS PLACE IS NOT.** Every previous attempt produced one of these.
  1. NOT a flat plain. 2. NOT terraces or stepped platforms — that is the FIRST region's signature.
  3. NOT strata, sediment or cross-sections. 4. NOT an archive, library or vault.
  5. NOT a formless tangle or an empty void — a player has to be able to navigate it.

**THIS REGION.** Because nothing is ever removed, the world has grown **upward** — a standing
structure of everything it has kept, rising out of the old plain in vast branching masses with open
air between them.

  · Enormous vertical forms, hundreds of metres, branching and rejoining, with **flyable gaps** and
    open sky between and above.
  · The old plain is still visible **far below**, small and pale, as the floor of the world.
  · The structures are grown rather than built — organic, load-bearing, thickening downward.
  · **Ledges, shelves and hollows** at many heights, where creatures rest and where a probe can stop.
  · Light comes from above and falls between the masses in long shafts, so height reads instantly.

REFERENCE: kelp forest columns; the standing stalks of a giant termite city; mangrove roots
photographed from below. **NOT a canyon and NOT a mountain range** — those are cut, and this grew.

**Register: TOWERING AND OPEN.** After two flat worlds, the first thing the player feels is that there
is an ABOVE.

THREE IMAGES: 1 · THE PLACE, no creatures, human outline for scale, probe small in the gaps.
2 · THE LONG ONE, stage three, **spanning between two vertical masses like a bridge** — a route.
3 · THE BACKWARD-LOOKING ONE, stage three, standing on a high shelf, its mantle hanging over the edge.


╔═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╗
║ PROMPT B — THE COLOSSAL.   The world stayed, the creatures became terrain. By SCALE.           ║
╚═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╝
[PASTE THE SHARED PREAMBLE, THEN THIS]

**THIS REGION.** The ground is recognisably the second world's, and everything standing on it has
become **enormous** — the creatures are now the landscape, and the player flies among them.

  · The stage-three animals are **hundreds of metres long**. Their backs are ridgelines, their plated
    flanks are cliffs, the gaps between their limbs are canyons.
  · Smaller creatures live **on** them, and the passengers are now the size of houses.
  · The player navigates BY them: fly along a spine, under an arch of ribs, through the gap where two
    of them lie against each other.
  · The old plain is far below and mostly hidden beneath the bodies standing on it.
  · Strong low directional light, so the animals cast **kilometre-long shadows** that are themselves
    part of the terrain.

REFERENCE: the scale of a blue whale beside a diver; sleeping dragons used as geography; a beached
leviathan the size of a hill. **NOT a boneyard and NOT ruins** — everything here is alive.

**Register: DWARFING AND CALM.** The creatures are not threatening. They are simply the size of
country, and they were the size of horses two worlds ago.

THREE IMAGES: 1 · THE PLACE, the landscape made of living bodies, human outline microscopic, probe
tiny between them. 2 · THE LONG ONE, stage three, **its full length crossing the entire frame as a
horizon**. 3 · THE BACKWARD-LOOKING ONE, stage three, one animal filling the frame, its mantle a
hanging cliff of plates with passengers working across it like climbers.


╔═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╗
║ PROMPT C — THE INTERIOR.   The player is now INSIDE something. By ENCLOSURE.                    ║
╚═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╝
[PASTE THE SHARED PREAMBLE, THEN THIS]

**THIS REGION.** The first two worlds were outdoors under an open sky. This one has a **ceiling**. The
player has flown into the inside of something enormous that keeps everything, and the whole region is
one continuous interior.

  · A vast enclosed volume with a visible **roof far overhead**, walls at the edges of sight, and a
    floor — a chamber the size of a landscape.
  · Structure at many heights: galleries, buttresses, spans and openings between chambers, all
    **flyable**.
  · The light is **internal** — it comes from the structure itself and from the creatures, not from a
    sky. Warm amber pooling in the deep parts, cool pale light from high openings.
  · It is grown, not constructed: ribbed, chambered, organic. Somewhere between an inhabited body and
    a cave system that decided on its shape.
  · The old outdoor plain is glimpsed once, through a far opening, tiny and pale — the way out.

REFERENCE: the inside of a cathedral-sized geode; the chambered interior of a nautilus; a cave system
lit from within. **NOT a machine interior and NOT a spaceship** — no panels, no corridors, no doors.

**Register: ENCLOSED AND ENORMOUS.** The player has gone INSIDE the thing they were walking on for two
worlds, and it is bigger inside than it was outside.

THREE IMAGES: 1 · THE PLACE, no creatures, the full interior with its roof and its far openings, human
outline for scale, probe flying between galleries. 2 · THE LONG ONE, stage three, **coiled through
several chambers at once**, passing through openings — one animal occupying the architecture.
3 · THE BACKWARD-LOOKING ONE, stage three, in a high gallery, its mantle falling through an opening
into the chamber below.

JUDGING THE NINE

  THE THIRD-STAGE TEST Put each creature beside the attachment. Same animal, hugely escalated? If it
                       reads as a different species the lineage broke; if it reads as the same size
                       and weight, nothing evolved.
