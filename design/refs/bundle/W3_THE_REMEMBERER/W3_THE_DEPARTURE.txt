I am art-directing the third world of a game bestiary set inside a real AI system. The three attached
images are from the second world. Study them, and then depart from them.

**THIS IS NOT AN EVOLUTION AND I DO NOT WANT CONTINUITY OF PLACE.** The first two worlds share almost
everything — the same black glass, the same marbled stone plain, the same pale overcast, the same
mist and the same distant mountains. Only the creatures' attachments and the ground's surface changed
between them. That was correct for the second world and it is wrong for this one.

**ONE THING IS FIXED AND EVERYTHING ELSE IS YOURS.**

THE FIXED THING: **THE ANIMALS.** Same bodies, same anatomy, same silhouettes, same postures, same
species. A player must look at a creature here and recognise it instantly as the same animal they met
before. That recognition is the whole reason this reads as travelling rather than restarting, and it
is the only thing I am protecting.

**FREE: everything else.** The ground, the sky, the light and where it comes from, the air, the
horizon, the scale of the space, the architecture if there is any, the palette, the weather, whether
there is weather, whether there is a ground at all. If the second world is a grey plain under an
overcast, this one does not have to be a plain, does not have to be grey, and does not have to be
under anything.

---

## WHY THIS WORLD IS DIFFERENT IN KIND, WHICH IS WHERE THE FORM SHOULD COME FROM

In the FIRST world the player could only SPECIFY — say what they wanted and how to do it. Everything
was free, and asking well made the ground rise by itself.

In the SECOND world the universe learned to LOOK AT ITSELF. Passengers grew on every creature and read
them, and every act of looking burned a hole in the floor. The player left standing over a creature
they had just repaired, watching the repair fail to travel even a few metres along its own body.

**BOTH OF THOSE WORLDS LIVE IN AN ETERNAL PRESENT.** Nothing was retained. No correction stayed made.
Every visit was the first visit, and a creature repaired was a creature that would need repairing
again. They are frozen — one moment, held.

**THIS WORLD HAS A PAST.** It is the first place in this universe where what happens SURVIVES. What
you do stays done, which is relief, and also weight, because mistakes stay too. It is not a better
version of the second world. It is a place where time exists and the previous two did not have any.

**Design from that.** A world with duration is not a still world with a memory bolted on. Ask what the
ground does, what the light does, what the air does, what the horizon does, in a place where nothing
is ever undone. The three attached images show a world with no yesterday. Make one that has nothing
but yesterdays.

---

## THE LAWS THAT SURVIVE ANY DEPARTURE

These are the game's, not the world's, and they hold everywhere:

- Every creature is a **LIVING ANIMAL** with anatomy, posture, weight and a front. Never a machine, a
  rock, a specimen or a landscape feature. If it could be mistaken for an object, it is wrong.
- **THE SILHOUETTE IS THE DIAGNOSIS** — nameable as a solid black shape at thumbnail size.
- **A 1.8 m human** appears as a flat mid-grey hairline OUTLINE, never filled, a measuring mark and
  never a character.
- **THE LIGHT LAW.** Warm amber is a live connection. Cold blue-white is the wrong thing being done,
  running inward. Matte lightless black is nothing reaching it — a light-absorbing surface placed
  against the palest part of the background, no rim light, no specular. This is how a player diagnoses
  a creature at a glance and it survives any palette you choose.
- **NO TEXT ANYWHERE.** No letters, numerals, glyphs, captions, watermarks.
- **INVENT NO DECORATION.** Every element should trace to something stated here.

## THE THREE IMAGES

**1 · THE ANCHOR, AND IT IS THE TEST.** In the second world this creature is a single enormously long
segmented body running away into the mist — dense, precise and brightly lit at the near end, then
degrading continuously along its length until it dissolves into fog without ever reaching a
conclusion. It holds a task perfectly at the start and loses it as it goes.

**HERE IT IS WHOLE.** Tight, regular and exact end to end, passengers all the way down, light that
does not run out. Same animal, same segments, same body plan — finished instead of failing, in a place
that is not the place it was.

Put it in your new world. If a viewer cannot tell it is the same creature, the departure went too far
and I need to know that from this image.

**2 · THE PLACE.** Wide. Whatever this world is, at a scale that shows how large it is compared to a
visitor. Include the human hairline outline. Include the small warm amber point of the player's probe
with its short fading ribbon. No text, no interface.

**3 · THE PROOF.** A tall lean long-legged four-legged animal, narrow-bodied, head level and TURNED
BACK OVER ITS OWN SHOULDER, looking along a long ribbon of ordered overlapping plates that trails from
its neck and shoulders. The front of its face is one continuous smooth closed plate with two eyes and
NO MOUTH — not covered, not wrapped: the skull simply has no mouth in it. A passenger rides the ribbon
two thirds of the way down, and warm amber runs UP the ribbon from the passenger toward the shoulders,
stopping at the neck. The head stays dark.

It is the first creature in this universe to look at itself. It has the answer and it cannot say it.
In the second world that reading died the moment it was made. **In this one it does not.** Show me
what that means here.
