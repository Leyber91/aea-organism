THE PRINCIPLE BEHIND ALL THREE, AND IT IS WHY THE LAST ATTEMPTS FAILED:

**A REGION IS BUILT FOR THE SKILL THE PLAYER JUST GAINED.**

  Region 1 · the player learned to SPECIFY. The ground rose wherever they asked well.
  Region 2 · the player learned to LOOK. Every act of looking burned a hole in the floor.
  Region 3 · the player has learned to CARRY. **So this region must be built out of distance.**

Carrying means nothing unless there is somewhere to carry TO. The first two regions were **one flat
undifferentiated plain** — everywhere was the same place, so travel had no meaning and nothing needed
transporting. That is the real reason they felt alike, and changing the colour of the plain would not
have fixed it.

**REGION 3 IS THE FIRST PLACE IN THIS GAME WITH GEOGRAPHY.** Distinct locations, genuinely far apart,
each unlike the others, with real distance between them — and the whole point of the region is moving
something from one to another and having it still be there when you return.

Three answers to what that geography is. Each is a different SHAPE of distance.

I am art-directing the third region of a game set inside a real AI system. The two attached images are
creatures from the second region. **The creatures carry over. The place does not.**

**THE PLAYER.** A small craft called THE PROBE — a single point of warm amber light with a fading
amber ribbon behind it, flying low. Never a designed vehicle, always small in frame. The player flies
through this region, and **this is the first region where flying somewhere means arriving somewhere
different.**

**WHAT THE PLAYER CAN NOW DO, AND WHY THE PLACE LOOKS LIKE THIS.** In the first region they could only
SPECIFY — ask well, and the ground rose. In the second they learned to LOOK — and every act of looking
burned a hole in the floor, and nothing they fixed stayed fixed. **In this region what happens
SURVIVES.** They can take something from one place to another and it is still there when they come
back. So this region is made of **somewhere and elsewhere**, and of the distance between them.

**THIS MUST BE A PLAYABLE REGION**, not a landscape painting:
  · **several DISTINCT locations**, each visibly a different kind of place, far enough apart to be a
    journey
  · **routes between them** a player can see and follow — and the gaps that make routes matter
  · **verticality.** The first two regions were flat and it cost them
  · **landmarks** visible from a long way off, so a player can navigate by sight
If a viewer cannot point at two different places and the way between them, the image has failed.

**THE CREATURES — third stage of the same lineage.** These are the attached animals, escalated. Stage
one to two is a modest step; **stage two to three is enormous** — the same animal, instantly
recognisable, massively bigger, armoured, elaborated, carrying far more. Do not redesign them.

  **THE LONG ONE** (attached 1). Stage two: a segmented body degrading along its length, coming apart,
  never reaching a conclusion. **STAGE THREE: whole and enormous** — every segment exact end to end,
  armoured and plated, its passengers ranked along it in order. It no longer lies on the landscape:
  **it connects places.** A player can fly along it.

  **THE BACKWARD-LOOKING ONE** (attached 2). Keep the sealed face: one continuous smooth plate, two
  eyes, **NO MOUTH**. Stage three is far larger and heavier, its trailing ribbon now a **vast mantle
  of ranked plates** with many passengers working it, and the amber reaching **past the neck into the
  head** for the first time. It has the answer, it can now see where the answer came from, and it
  still cannot say it.

**THE LIGHT LAW — unchanged, and it is how a player diagnoses at a glance.** Warm amber is a live
connection. Cold blue-white is the wrong thing being done, running INWARD, never out into the air.
Matte lightless black is nothing reaching it — no rim light, no specular, read as outline only.

**RULES.** Every creature is a LIVING ANIMAL with anatomy, posture, weight and a front. Silhouette is
the diagnosis, nameable in black at thumbnail size. A 1.8 m human appears as a flat mid-grey hairline
OUTLINE, never filled — a measuring mark, never a character. No text anywhere. Invent no decoration.
Landscape, roughly 3:2.

**SIX THINGS THIS REGION IS NOT.** Every previous attempt produced one.
  1. NOT a flat plain — that is regions 1 and 2 and it is spent.
  2. NOT terraces or stepped platforms — region 1's signature.
  3. NOT strata, sediment or cross-sections.
  4. NOT an archive, library or vault.
  5. NOT a formless tangle or a void — a player must be able to navigate it.
  6. NOT one continuous surface. **There must be separate places.**

**THIS REGION.** Separate masses, each a complete small world, hanging in open space with real
emptiness between them. Getting from one to another is a crossing, and crossings are where the game
lives.

  · **Six or eight distinct masses**, each visibly different — one dense and dark, one bright and
    open, one broken, one still growing — at different heights and distances.
  · Genuine **void** between them: air, light, nothing to stand on. Distance you can see.
  · **The stage-three long creature is a BRIDGE** between two of them — the only fixed route in
    sight, and a player can fly its length. Everything else is a leap.
  · Each mass has its own light. One glows amber from within, another is matte and unlit.
  · No ground plane anywhere in frame. Up and down are both open.

REFERENCE: floating islands with real air between them; a cluster of seed pods on separate stalks;
tidal islets at high water. **NOT an asteroid field** — these are alive and inhabited, not debris.

**Register: OPEN AND DELIBERATE.** For the first time, going somewhere is a decision with a cost.

THREE IMAGES: 1 · THE REGION, no creatures — several distinct masses, the void between them, the
probe crossing, human outline on the nearest for scale. 2 · THE LONG ONE, stage three, **spanning the
gap between two masses as a living bridge**, passengers ranked along its back. 3 · THE
BACKWARD-LOOKING ONE, stage three, on the edge of one mass with its mantle hanging over the drop.


╔═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╗
║ PROMPT B — THE BRANCHING.   Distance is a NETWORK. Places joined by channels.                  ║
╚═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╝
[PASTE THE SHARED PREAMBLE, THEN THIS]

**THIS REGION.** Places connected by **visible channels** — grown vessels, tubes, spans that carry
things between them. The region is a network you read like a map, and the routes are as much a place
as the nodes.

  · **Distinct chambers and nodes**, each a different kind of place, joined by branching channels of
    varying thickness. Thick ones are old and heavily used; thin ones lead somewhere rarely visited.
  · The channels are **flyable**: open-topped, or wide enough to enter. Following one is how you
    travel.
  · **Warm amber moves along the busiest channels** — the game's live-connection colour, doing
    literal work as traffic. Dead branches are matte and unlit.
  · Nodes sit at different heights, so the network is three-dimensional, not a plan.
  · Something is always in transit. The region is never still.

REFERENCE: a fungal mycelium at architectural scale; a river delta seen from above but built in three
dimensions; the vessels of a leaf enlarged to a country. **NOT pipes and NOT a machine** — grown, not
plumbed.

**Register: CONNECTED AND BUSY.** The first two regions were inert. This one is circulating.

THREE IMAGES: 1 · THE REGION, no creatures — the network from a height, nodes at different levels,
amber moving in the main channels, probe entering one. 2 · THE LONG ONE, stage three, **inside a major
channel, filling it, itself the traffic** — a player flying alongside it. 3 · THE BACKWARD-LOOKING
ONE, stage three, at a junction where several channels meet, its mantle spread across the meeting.


╔═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╗
║ PROMPT C — THE DESCENT.   Distance is DEPTH. Places stacked at different levels.               ║
╚═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╝
[PASTE THE SHARED PREAMBLE, THEN THIS]

**THIS REGION.** A single enormous vertical shaft or gorge with **distinct inhabited levels** down its
walls, each a different world, connected by ledges, spans and open drops. You travel by descending and
climbing, and how deep you are is where you are.

  · **Four or five clearly different levels**, visible at once from the right vantage: the bright
    upper terraces open to the sky, a dim middle band, a warm amber-lit deep, and a dark floor barely
    visible below.
  · Each level is inhabited differently and looks different. **The deeper it is, the older and denser
    what it holds.**
  · Real **openings between levels** — shafts, gaps, spans — that a probe can fly through.
  · Light falls from a single opening far above and weakens with depth, so **depth reads instantly**
    and warm amber from below becomes the only light in the deepest parts.
  · This is NOT layers of rock. **It is levels of a place**, each inhabited, each somewhere you go.

REFERENCE: a vast inhabited sinkhole; the open shaft of a cave system with settlements down its walls;
a canyon whose walls are lived in. **NOT strata and NOT a cross-section** — you are inside it, not
looking at a diagram of it.

**Register: DEEP AND ORIENTED.** For the first time the player always knows where they are, because
depth tells them.

THREE IMAGES: 1 · THE REGION, no creatures — the full shaft from partway down, several inhabited
levels visible at once, light from far above, amber from far below, probe descending. 2 · THE LONG
ONE, stage three, **spanning the shaft horizontally between two levels** — a crossing where there was
none. 3 · THE BACKWARD-LOOKING ONE, stage three, standing on a deep ledge, its mantle hanging down
into the level below.
