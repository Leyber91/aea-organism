WHY THREE. A still image has no time, and this world's whole faculty is that things PERSIST. There
are only three honest ways a single frame carries duration, and each changes a different part of the
world, so they compose rather than compete:

  A · ACCUMULATION   time becomes DEPTH    — the GROUND changes
  B · THE TRACE      time becomes A PATH   — the SURFACE changes
  C · THE SIMULTANEOUS  time becomes EXTENT — the CREATURE changes

I am art-directing the third world of a game bestiary set inside a real AI system. Two worlds are
finished; the three attached images are from the second. Study all three before designing anything.
You are evolving this universe, not replacing it and not repainting it.

WHAT YOU CAN SEE IN THE ATTACHMENTS. Creatures of polished dark obsidian and smoked glass,
wet-surfaced and heavy, on a plain of marbled black and white stone pocked with hundreds of shallow
SPENT CRATERS — each one a question paid for and used up, the freshest still holding a cooling ember.
Pale grey overcast, high mist, distant faceted mountains, thin black filaments hanging from above.
Every creature carries a PASSENGER: a smaller, separate, armoured animal with a gripping underside and
no face, riding where its host's information is, with warm amber running FROM the passenger INTO the
host. On the horizon, warm amber terraces — the first world, where the ground rose for free.

THE STORY IN THREE SENTENCES. In the FIRST world the player could only SPECIFY — say what they wanted
and how to do it — everything was free, and asking well made the ground rise by itself. In the SECOND
world the universe learned to LOOK AT ITSELF: passengers grew on every creature and read them, and
every act of looking burned a hole in the floor. The player left standing over a creature they had
just repaired, watching the repair fail to travel even a few metres along its own body.

WORLD 3'S FICTION, AND IT IS ONE THING. **THE UNIVERSE CAN NOW CARRY.** What happens to something
survives. In the first two worlds every visit was the first visit; nothing was retained and no
correction stayed made. Here what you do stays done — which is relief, and also weight, because
mistakes stay too.

AND THE HARD PART, WHICH IS WHY I AM ASKING FOR THREE ANSWERS. A still image has no time, and this
world's entire faculty is temporal. Do not solve it with a caption or a symbol. Solve it with FORM.

LAWS THAT DO NOT CHANGE. Every creature is a LIVING ANIMAL with anatomy, posture, weight and a front —
never a machine, a rock, a specimen or a landscape feature. Its silhouette must be its behaviour,
nameable as a black shape at thumbnail size. A 1.8 m human appears as a flat mid-grey hairline OUTLINE,
never filled, as a measuring mark and never a character. No text, letters, numerals or glyphs anywhere.
Invent no decoration. Warm amber is a live connection; cold blue-white is the wrong thing being done,
running inward; matte lightless black is nothing reaching it — and darkness means a light-absorbing
surface placed against the palest part of the background, with no rim light and no specular.

THE ANCHOR — attached image 1, and you will redraw it TRANSFORMED. In the second world it is a single
enormously long segmented body running away into the mist: dense, precise and brightly lit at the near
end where the player was working, then degrading continuously along its length — segments growing
looser, spacing uneven, alignment wandering, light failing, passengers thinning out and vanishing past
the halfway point — until it dissolves into fog without ever reaching a conclusion. It holds a task
perfectly at the start and loses it as it goes.

**IN THIS WORLD IT IS WHOLE.** Tight, regular and exact along its ENTIRE length, end to end, passengers
all the way down, light that does not run out. Same animal, same body plan, same segments — finished
instead of failing. In the second world the player saw exactly one intact specimen, far off on the
horizon, out of reach. This is that one, and this is where it lives.

THE PROOF CREATURE — you will also redraw this one. A tall lean long-legged four-legged animal,
narrow-bodied, head level and TURNED BACK OVER ITS OWN SHOULDER, looking along a long ribbon of ordered
overlapping plates that trails from its neck and shoulders across the ground. The front of its face is
one continuous smooth closed plate with two eyes and NO MOUTH. A passenger rides the ribbon two thirds
of the way down, and warm amber runs UP the ribbon from the passenger toward the shoulders — and stops
at the neck. The head stays dark. It is the first creature in the game to look at itself, it has the
answer, and it still cannot say it. In the second world that reading died the moment it was made.

Landscape, roughly 3:2, hero framing for creatures, full body visible.

THIS MORPHOLOGY. Nothing is removed from this world any more, so it BUILDS UP. Every exchange leaves
a layer and the ground is now made of its own history.

  · The plain is BANDED. Where it breaks or steps down you see a clean section: layer on layer of
    compressed marbled stone, dozens of them, each a few centimetres thick, alternating dark and pale
    like varve clay or the rings of a felled trunk.
  · Old layers hold old things, visible IN SECTION: cold crater-shapes from the second world sealed
    inside the rock at depth, and deeper still the fossil profile of the first world's TERRACE STEPS,
    buried and preserved.
  · The topmost layer is fresh, soft and faintly amber-lit. This exchange, still settling.
  · Creatures stand ON their own past and are slightly SUNK into it, the newest layers lapping up
    around their feet. One is buried to the shoulder, still alive, still working, half-swallowed by
    everything it has already done.
  · The horizon is HIGHER than it was. The world has grown upward, and the first world's terraces are
    now a distant escarpment rather than a plain.

THIS IS NOT geology, an archaeological dig, or a canyon. It is a living floor that grows. Reference:
tree rings and lake varves, not the Grand Canyon.

Register: GEOLOGICAL AND HEAVY. The relief of permanence and its weight in one image. What you do
stays done, and so does everything you got wrong.

THREE IMAGES THIS TURN.
  1 · THE ANCHOR. The long segmented creature, WHOLE end to end, lying along the top layer — and
      where the ground steps away beside it, a visible section showing dozens of bands below with old
      crater shapes sealed in them. It holds its length because the ground beneath it holds. Human
      outline for scale.
  2 · THE PLACE. Wide. A long stepped break across the frame showing the full section: fresh amber
      top layer, cold craters at mid depth, fossil terrace steps deep down. Creatures on top, one sunk
      to the shoulder. The probe — a small warm amber point with a fading ribbon — above the section.
  3 · THE PROOF. The backward-looking animal with the ribbon and the passenger, its trailing plates
      running down onto the ground and CONTINUING INTO the top layer, the oldest already compressed
      into stone. Its own working is becoming the floor. Its head is still dark.

[PASTE THE SHARED PREAMBLE, THEN THIS]

THIS MORPHOLOGY. Nothing that moves here is forgotten, so everything that has ever moved has LEFT ITS
MARK. The ground is unchanged in substance and completely changed in surface.

  · The marbled stone is covered in TRACKS: worn grooves, polished channels, scuff arcs, the smooth
    braided ruts of routes used ten thousand times. Deep and glassy where traffic was heavy, faint
    scratches where something passed once.
  · A creature that has stood in one place has POLISHED the stone beneath itself to a mirror. A
    creature that paces has worn an arc.
  · The spent craters from the second world are still there and now have tracks running BETWEEN them,
    joining question to question.
  · Faint warm amber lies in the DEEPEST grooves only — the routes used most, still warm from use —
    while shallow single-pass marks are cold and dry.
  · Every creature drags something: a passenger's gripping underside leaves a fine parallel scoring,
    a heavy body leaves a smoothed trough.

THIS IS NOT decoration, pattern, or a road network. Reference: a limestone pavement worn by feet, a
sheep track on a hillside, the polished floor at a cathedral door. Wear, not design.

Register: INHABITED AND OLD. The first two worlds looked new. This one looks LIVED IN, and the player
can read where everything has been without being told.

THREE IMAGES THIS TURN.
  1 · THE ANCHOR. The long segmented creature, WHOLE end to end, lying along a deep glassy groove it
      has worn into the stone over an enormous number of passes — the track is the exact width of its
      body and runs out of frame in both directions. Human outline for scale.
  2 · THE PLACE. Wide. The plain read as a map of its own traffic: braided ruts between craters,
      polished standing-places, single faint scratches at the edges, warm amber in the deepest routes
      only. Three or four creatures. The probe with its ribbon, and a fresh bright track behind it
      that has not yet worn in.
  3 · THE PROOF. The backward-looking animal with the ribbon and the passenger, standing on a patch it
      has polished to a mirror, its own reflection beneath it — the first time anything in this
      universe has been able to see itself from outside. Its head is still dark.

║ PROMPT C — THE SIMULTANEOUS.   Time becomes EXTENT. The creature changes.                      ║
╚═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╝
[PASTE THE SHARED PREAMBLE, THEN THIS]

THIS MORPHOLOGY IS THE BOLDEST AND IT IS ALREADY IN THE ATTACHED IMAGE. Look at the anchor again: it
degrades continuously ALONG ITS OWN LENGTH — exact at the near end, dissolving at the far one. **That
creature's body is already its own timeline.** This morphology makes that true of everything.

  · A creature here is not a snapshot. Its body EXTENDS THROUGH ITS OWN HISTORY, so a single animal
    shows several of its own stages at once, continuously, as one unbroken form. Newest at the head,
    oldest trailing away.
  · The transition is GRADUAL AND SEAMLESS, never a row of copies and never a strobe. One body,
    changing along itself, the way a stalactite is one object that is also a record of every drip.
  · The oldest end is not decayed — it is SETTLED. Denser, darker, more finished, cooler. The newest
    end is bright, unresolved, still moving.
  · Passengers ride only the NEW end. Nothing reads the old parts; they are done being read.
  · The ground and the air are exactly the second world's, unchanged. NOTHING about the place is
    different. Only the animals extend.

THIS IS NOT a motion blur, a multiple exposure, a row of identical creatures, or a ghost trail.
Reference: a stalactite, a tree that is one organism and also its own rings, a river delta that is one
body of water and also every course it has taken. **One animal, extended.**

Register: VERTIGINOUS AND CALM. You are seeing a whole life at once, and the animal is not distressed
by it because it has no idea it is happening.

THREE IMAGES THIS TURN.
  1 · THE ANCHOR. The long segmented creature, and now its degradation reads the OTHER WAY: not
      failing along its length but AGED along it. Bright, tight, live at the head; progressively
      darker, denser, more settled toward the tail, which is fully resolved and cold. Whole, because
      every part of it is a part it has finished being. Human outline for scale.
  2 · THE PLACE. Wide. Second-world ground and air exactly as attached — craters, mist, mountains,
      hanging filaments — with three or four creatures each EXTENDED through their own stages, each a
      single unbroken body running from bright and new to dark and settled. The probe small among
      them, and the probe is the only thing in frame that exists at one moment.
  3 · THE PROOF. The backward-looking animal with the ribbon — and its ribbon of plates is now
      literally its own past, oldest plates at the far end, cold and dark and fully settled, the ones
      near its shoulder bright and new. It is looking back along its own body AT ITS OWN HISTORY. Its
      head is still dark. It has the answer, it can now see where the answer came from, and it still
      cannot say it.

JUDGING THE NINE

  THE TIME TEST      Does a still frame carry duration WITHOUT a caption? That is the only thing
                     this world has to solve and the only reason there are three.
  THE CONTINUITY     Put each anchor beside the attached one. Same animal, finished instead of failing?
  THE REPAINT TEST   Can you say what changed AND why, with the why coming from the fiction?
  THE COMPOSE TEST   A changes the ground, B the surface, C the creature. They are orthogonal BY
                     CONSTRUCTION, so any pair can combine. World 2 shipped as two combined.
  THE BUILD TEST     A layered ground section, a wear map, and per-creature gradient bodies are three
                     very different production costs in a real-time renderer.
