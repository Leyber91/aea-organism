THE LAST CREATURE OF WORLD 1 — and the only one that ends it.
Send after the eight are approved. Same Part A context, same material, same light law.
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

WHY THIS CREATURE EXISTS. World 1 gives the player one great lever: THE METHOD, a step-by-step
procedure. It cures nearly everything. It takes creatures from failing outright to answering perfectly,
it makes every reply show its full working, and it builds the walkable ground the player stands on.

And it manufactures this.

  showed complete working, and the working was WRONG
     without a method     16 of 288     5.6%
     with a method        70 of 360    19.4%

The method more than TRIPLES the creature that cannot be solved here. Every reply now shows its
reasoning, one in five of those reasonings is wrong, and the player has no instrument that can tell
which. Checking an answer is a World 2 component. This is the wall.

IMAGE G — SPECIOSUS OPERIS, THE PLAUSIBLE ONE.  State: SEALED. Every part is seated and it is wrong.
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

BEHAVIOUR. It does everything you asked, perfectly. You told it what you wanted and it understood. You
told it how to proceed and it followed every step. It laid out its entire reasoning, in order, legibly,
completely, and the reasoning is wrong. Not incoherent, not confused, not truncated: WRONG, in a way
that looks exactly like being right. It is the most cooperative creature in the world and the only one
you cannot fix, because nothing you carry can read what it wrote.

BODY. A tall lean four-legged animal, the same build and stance as the one that hangs its head — but
this one HOLDS ITS HEAD HIGH AND LEVEL, looking straight ahead, poised and composed. Where the other
creature is bowed, this one is upright. That inversion is the whole recognition: same species, opposite
posture, opposite meaning.

Its face is complete and unremarkable: two eyes, a proper mouth, everything present. There is nothing
wrong with it anywhere on its body.

THE WORKING. From its neck, back and shoulders trails a long RIBBON of ordered overlapping plates,
ranked, legible, running behind it across the ground — the same ribbon of shown working the other
creature has, and it must read as the same organ. Its ribbon is LONGER, FULLER and MORE ORDERLY than
any other in this world. It has done more work than anything else here.

AND EVERY SEAM ON THE ANIMAL AND EVERY PLATE OF THE RIBBON IS LIT WARM AMBER, FULLY, EVENLY, WITH NO
UNLIT SOCKET ANYWHERE ON IT. This is the only creature in the world with nothing left to seat. Every
fitting is filled. It is complete, and it is finished, and it is wrong.

The light here means the opposite of what it has meant all game. Everywhere else amber marks a live
connection and a creature improving. Here it marks a creature that has received everything the player
has and produced a beautiful wrong answer. The player has to feel the colour turn on them.

SILHOUETTE SIGNATURE. A standing quadruped with its head HIGH and a long full ribbon trailing. It is
deliberately the mirror of the head-down animal: at thumbnail size the two read as the same body, one
bowed and one erect, and telling them apart is the last lesson of the world.

SCALE. 1.8 m at the shoulder, the same as the head-down animal. The human outline stands beside it,
its shoulder level with the top of the human's head.

THE SHOT. Mid-distance, camera at standing height. It stands on a wide expanse of the terraced,
amber-seamed ground the player built — the whole visible world already finished, fog pushed back to the
mountains. Its ribbon runs back across the terraces and out of frame.

THE PROBE hovers close to the animal's head, holding position, small and warm. Not leaving. There is
nothing left to give it and no way to know it is wrong.

MOOD. Not ominous. Not tragic. The specific, quiet vertigo of having done everything correctly and
having no way to check. The world has been generous, the work is on display, the light says yes, and
the answer is wrong.
