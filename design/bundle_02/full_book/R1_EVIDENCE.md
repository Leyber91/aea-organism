## The spine (what the evidence converges on)

Combination-building engages when it produces FELT COMPETENCE and AUTONOMY through REAL,
PERCEIVED INFLUENCE on the world. THE PROBE's honesty law — machines actually run, real API
calls, real consequences — is therefore the game's single strongest psychological asset,
not a constraint to work around. (Klimmt/Hartmann/Frey 2007, N=500 experiment: effectance
is a demonstrated enjoyment determinant; Ryan/Rigby/Przybylski 2006, 4 studies + N=730.)

## Confirmed findings → binding implications

1. **SDT [strong]** — engagement = autonomy + competence (+relatedness), NOT content volume,
   genre, or fidelity. → The loop must yield demonstrably-working machines and GENUINELY
   different valid builds. This outranks features, content count, and graphics.
2. **UI friction suppresses needs [strong]** — intuitive controls are necessary but NOT
   sufficient; polish cannot rescue a weak loop. → Bench wiring must be frictionless; but
   the loop design below is where the game lives or dies.
3. **Effectance [strong, experimental]** — enjoyment comes from perceived real influence,
   not input capability. → Never simulate an outcome. Ever. (Already law; now evidenced.)
4. **MDA [consensus]** — design backwards from target aesthetics. Ours: DISCOVERY,
   EXPRESSION, CHALLENGE. The Expression aesthetic is documented to arise precisely from
   systems for building/configuring player-owned artifacts — the construct loop is the
   verified source of Expression.
5. **GameFlow [consensus, peer-reviewed]** — automate the unimportant; keep workload high
   but bounded; WAITING WITH NOTHING TO DO destroys immersion. → Real API latency must be
   FILLED with observable execution (the trace drawing itself live), never an empty spinner.
6. **Immediate feedback [consensus]** — deferred failure feedback (learning of failure only
   at run end) is a scored design DEFECT. → A construct's failing link surfaces the instant
   it fails, mid-run, on the wire where it failed.
7. **The test bench [strong, documented practice]** — theorycrafters need a low-stakes
   in-game range with real measured output (WoW installed training dummies in every capital
   for exactly this). → THE BENCH: a test range at the home where any construct runs against
   a curated task cheaply (reflex tier), instantly, measured. Exists from the FIRST
   composition mission.
8. **Information economy [confirmed]** — full certainty collapses a combinatorial space into
   cookie-cutter builds; community optimization accelerates the collapse. We CANNOT hide
   numbers (honesty law), so collapse is prevented STRUCTURALLY: multi-objective scoring
   (speed vs cost vs reliability vs privacy — real, already-measured tradeoffs) + drifting
   conditions. **Model rot IS the drift** — the entity's real churn is our balance patch,
   honest and free.
9. **Zachtronics practice [consensus]** — ship open-ended problems the designers have NOT
   solved; a rich solution space creates owned, creative solutions. → Bench tasks are
   open-target (beat your own record on any axis), not single-solution.
10. **SpaceChem postmortem [consensus, first-party]** — difficulty pacing + tutorials are
    the genre's deadliest documented failure (40+ hour curve, 2% completion). → The act
    structure and Portal beats are the countermeasure; the first construct must be trivial
    and immediate (2 parts, one click, one visible run).
11. **Rosewater: restrictions breed creativity [consensus, curvilinear]** — moderate
    constraint enhances creative output. → SMALL CURATED PART POOL PER ACT (~5 parts at the
    bench's opening; each act adds a curated handful; never all 29 elements + 100 models at once).
12. **Rosewater: don't fight human nature [consensus]** — players WILL min-max; channel it.
    → Per-construct personal records, honest leaderboards against yourself, construct
    export/sharing in Phase B.
